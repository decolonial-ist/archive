FBIS Duma Election Report 18 Oct
WA1810011695

(FBIS Media Note] This is the fifth in a weekly series
of reports that FBIS will produce on the campaign for
the 17 December Russian Duma elections. It is based
solely on Russian media reporting.

Recent Developments

Signatures Pouring In for Major Parties The Central
Election Commission (CEC) today certified three more
parties as having obtained the 200,000 signatures nec-
essary to appear on the ballot: The Congress of Russian
Communities (KRO) led by Yuriy Skokov, Vladimir
Zhirinovskiy’s Liberal Democratic Party (LDPR), and
the Women of Russia (INTERFAX, 17 October). With
less than a week before the deadline, the CEC has reg-
istered four parties, including the Communist Party of
the Russian Federation (CPFR) registered earlier.

At least six other parties appear to have collected
sufficient signatures to be certified before the deadline:

¢ Agrarian Party (APR) Chairman Mikhail Lapshin
announced on 14 October that his party has col-
lected two million signatures and would submit
them on 16 October (ITAR-TASS).

* Russia Is Our Home (NDR) Executive Committee
Deputy Chairman Vladimir Ryzhkov asserted that
the NDR has collected 1.7 million signatures
(ITAR-TASS, 12 October).

¢ Ivan Rybkin said that his bloc has already garnered
600,000 signatures and planned to submit 300,000
of them to the CEC on 17 October (INTERFAX,
16 October).

¢ Russia’s Democratic Choice-United Democrats
has collected 200,000 signatures, but “as few as
80,000 signatures" had come into the Yabloko
bloc’s headquarters, according to KOMMER-
SANT-DAILY (10 October).

¢ Derzhava and the Unions and Industrialists of
Russia have also collected enough signatures to
register, according to SELSKAYA ZHIZN (17
October).

Yeltsin, NDR Leaders Hold Meeting President Boris
Yeltsin met with Premier Viktor Chernomyrdin and
other key leaders from NDR election bloc on 11
October to discuss the bloc’s campaign. State Property
Committee chief Sergey Belyayev, NDR’s campaign
director, told ITAR-TASS (11 October) that Yeltsin
set a "clear task" for the bloc: "Form a democratic
majority."

While noting that as the "guarantor" of social accord
the president cannot openly state a "preference" for any
party, Belyayev asserted that Yeltsin “expressed his own
political sympathies" during the meeting and that it was
“easier to list all those whom the president does not
accept.” According to Belyayev this list includes the
CPRF and all other communist fragments, the Agrarian
Party, and Vladimir Zhirinovskiy’s LDPR, which he
said Yeltsin “has never supported." As for the KRO,
led by Yuriy Skokov and General Aleksandr Lebed,
Belyayev said "we did not touch on it during the course
of our meeting.”

Grachev Not a Candidate Defense Minister Pavel
Grachev announced on 12 October that he “never
intended to stand" and is “not planning" to stand
as a Duma candidate (ITAR-TASS), directly refuting
Gen. Boris Gromov, one of his chief antagonists, who
claimed several days earlier that Grachev would stand
for election in one of Saratov Oblast’s single-seat
districts (see "FBIS Duma Election Report 10 Oct").

IZVESTIYA Details Campaign Costs Noting that the
cost of an election campaign is "a very well-kept
secret," IZVESTIYA published an article on 14 October
outlining the price range of several key components of
an average campaign:

¢ Signature collection by “professional services"
costs from $500-$5,000 per month, and the fee
“doubles” if a candidate wins. Taking into account
the support staff working under a professional
“consultant,” the campaign to win a single-seat
race “could cost the candidate $25,000-$150,000"
and “cases are known where candidates have paid
out up to $300,900."

¢ The price for the services of a "consultant psy-
chologist" are typically $40-$60 per hour and some
charge up to $200 per hour; “lecturers” who "in-
struct” parties and candidates on “all the subtleties
of election campaigning” earn $20-$40 per hour.

* Polling is calculated on the basis of $6-$12 per
questionnaire and sample sizes generally range
between 800-1,500 respondents. An average poll
“without analysis" costs approximately $6,000,
“with analysis" $9,000, and "in certain cases" the
price can reach $30,000.

¢ An average 30-second film “plug” costs $8,500;
a "film" of the same duration but with computer
graphics costs $5,000. An “information film"
or “feature item" lasting 3-10 minutes costs
$1,500-$2,500 if shot in Moscow, $2,000-$3,500
if shot in other Russian cities.

¢ One minute of airtime on Public Russian Televi-
sion (ORT) or “Channel 1" starts at $1,500 and

In the Provinces

Nozhikov Shuns Rossel’s Party Irkutsk Governor Yuriy
Nozhikov announced on 10 October that he will not join
Sverdlovsk Governor Eduard Rossel’s Transformation
of the Fatherland party. Nozhikov was reported to
be among the top three on its as yet unpublished
candidate list, but following an official visit to South
Korea with Prime Minister Chernomyrdin to discuss
a future project to deliver gas from Irkutsk, Nozhikov
announced that he “is not, was not, and will not be" a
member "of any bloc.” He added, however, that he will
support candidates from Rossel’s party and from the
Party of Russian Unity and Accord (SOVETSKAYA
MOLODEZH, 12 October).

NDR Experiencing Problems in Stavropol According
to SEVERNYY KAVKAZ, the NDR faces an uphill
election battle in Stavropol Kray. The weekly reported
on 7 September that Stavropol voters have an “active
dislike" for the bloc, which they blame for the war in
Chechnya, the economic "breakdown" in the country,
and the “bloody” events in the kray town of Buden-
novsk. As a result, the NDR "invariably" finishes “if
not last, then next to last" in opinion polls in the kray,
according to the paper.

Moreover, the party has suffered several recent defec-

SEVERNYY KAVKAZ also reported that all parties,
with the exception of the CPRF, have experienced
difficulties collecting signatures in the kray. Local
residents are apparently unwilling to stand in line to
offer their signatures as "they had two years before,"
and are actually “struggling in every way possible to
avoid this procedure." Even going door-to-door does
not appear to be working for signature collectors, one of
whom was reportedly beaten by an “emotional voter."
The difficulty in collecting signatures has, according to
the weekly, whittled down the number of announced
candidates in Stavropol Kray’s Stavropolskiy single-seat
district (Electoral District No. 55) from 19 to nine.

Parties Garnering Support in Kemerovo Mines Sev-
eral parties appear to be gaining political capital out
of mining strikes in the Kuzbass region of Kemerovo.
NEZAVISIMAYA GAZETA reported on 12 October
that the Independent Miners’ Trade Union is canvassing
striking miners in the region and holding election meet-
ings in support of Svyatoslav Fedorov’s Party of Work-
ers’ Self-Government. The union’s chairman, Sayetgali
Sharipov, is the party’s candidate in Kemerovo Oblast’s
Prokopyevskiy single-seat district (Electoral District No.
91). SEGODNYA reported on 11 October that “propa-
gandists" from the CPRF and the KRO have also been
“extremely active among the region’s miners."

A poll conducted by the Institute of Comparative Stud-
ies of Labor Relations and reported by NEZAVISI-
MAYA GAZETA seems to demonstrate that such ef-
forts have borne fruit. While Women of Russia led the
poll with 11.7 percent, the CPRF came in second with
6.2, and the Party of Workers’ Self-Government came
in third (no figure given). The same study showed Amar:
Tuleyev, chairman of the oblast’s legislature and num-
ber three on the CPRF candidate list, as the most popu-
lar political figure with 36.5 percent, followed by Svy-
atoslav Fedorov with 9.7 percent, and the KRO’s Alek-

Yavlinskiy Seeks To Boost Support in Krasnodar
Yabloko leader Grigoriy Yavlinskiy was on the cam-
paign hustings in Krasnodar Kray in early October in
hopes of improving his party’s standing in the popu-
lous region in southern Russia. Citing "preliminary" data
from a recent local poll, NEZAVISIMAYA GAZETA
(10 October) reported that Yabloko is tied for fourth
among Krasnodar’s voters with NDR behind the front-
running CPRF, the Agrarian Party, and the LDPR (no
specific figures were given).

According to NEZAVISIMAYA GAZETA those most
likely to vote for Yabloko in the kray are the "most
efficient people" between the ages of 30 and 50, who "as
a tule" are "state apparatus staffers, heads of privately-
owned structures, and specialists in the humanities" with
advanced degrees earning between 200,000 and 500,000
rubles [R]. Yavlinskiy reportedly said that Yabloko’s
electoral platform is "geared precisely to this audience."

that “no one has succeeded yet in gathering signatures

sign varies depending on the region and party or bloc
