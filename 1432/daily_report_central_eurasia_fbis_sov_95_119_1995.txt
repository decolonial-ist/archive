LD2106075595 Moscow ITAR-TASS in English
0436 GMT 21 Jun 95

["Press Review" — ITAR-TASS headline]

{FBIS Transcribed Text] MOSCOW June 21 (ITAR-
TASS) — Virtually all today’s leading newspapers print
reports from the route of Chechen terrorists’ convoy,
comment on the talks between the Russian government
delegation and representatives from separatist leader
Dzhokhar Dudayev in Groznyy and try to comprehend
the tragedy in the southern Russian city of Budennovsk,
especially actions by Russian authorities, the Kremlin
and the government.

The IZVESTIYA newspaper devotes nearly a whole
page to opinions on this topic expressed by member
cf the Presidential Council M. Chudakov, jurist Y.
Feofanov and publicist A. Plutnikov.

The same daily reports some particulars on functions
and powers of a just established interdepartmental
commission headed by Oleg Lobov, secretary of the
Russian National Security Council, on investigation of
reasons for the Budennovsk tragedy.

Analysing the situation after the Budennovsk tragedy,
the ARGUMENTY I FAKTY weekly writes: "The
Caucasus as well as other small ethnic groups received
a second Shamil (Shamil was a Chechen leader in the
war between Russia and North Caucasian peoples at the
time of Catherine II).

"It can be safely said that Dudayev does not exist any
more, starting from Wednesday or Thursday. Quite fan-
tastic versions are possible: some people may nomi-
nate Shamil Basayev as Chechen leader if elections are
called."

The same newspaper carries an interview with speaker
of the Federation Council (Russian parliament upper
chamber) Vladimir Shumeyko, in whose opinion Chech-
nya "has turned into an ordinary criminal zone over the
past three years. It did not resemble a state in any way.

“That criminal zone had to be destroyed, naturally with-
out infringing on human rights and without afflicting
civilians,” the speaker stresses.

At the same time, Shumeyko expresses concern that
Russia now has no streamlined national security system
which would conform to the scale of our state.

Under the headline "Don’t make heroes of rebels. Ter-
rorism is an armed baseness", the army newspaper Kras-
naya Zvezda writes in its leader: "Some are obviously
disinter:.sted in repeating an evident thing that animals
rather than people attacked Budennovsk.

"It is evidently more attractive, even in tragedy, to
have one’s own, ‘different’ from others, point of view.
As a result, commandos and interior troops are in the
forefront. Why did they attack? Where did they shoot?
Why did they rush as to a fire? It would be better
for them to wait until a kind man came and freed all
hostages without a single shot," the newspaper writes.

The Trud newspaper runs a feature by legislator
Vladimir Lukin, dealing with the Halifax summit. He
writes: "This world reckons with force (naturally not
only with military). The lot of the weak is to be on the
sidelines.

"The main thing is not to spend depleting prestige
for illusions and for quickly vanishing speechifying. A
responsible statesman should understand the importance
of the real weight of his country.”

The KOMSOMOLSKAYA PRAVDA newspaper
claims, referring to sources at the Russian Defence
Ministry, that units from the Moscow Military District
as well as cadets from the airborne forces school in
Ryazan started coming to Moscow.

According to the newspaper, some 40 pieces of armour
were in Moscow when the newspaper went to the press.
The daily explains that joint patrolling of the defence
and interior ministries’ troops is resumed in the Russian
capital.

The official reason is the events in Budennovsk and the
need for raising safety of Muscovites.

The ROSSIYSKAYA GAZETA newspaper carries a
commentary by Viktor Gordeyev a high-ranking official
from the Russian president’s office, on a federal bill on
amendments and addenda to the Russian criminal code,
which was submitted by President Boris Yeltsin to the
Lower House of the Russian Parliament.

The ROSSIYSKIYE VESTI newspaper claims that,
according to experts’ estimates, retail prices for milk
and butter may rise by 40 per cent and for sugar by 83
percent when customs duties on imports are imposed on
July 1.

Financial expert Ivan Zhagel comments in IZVESTIYA
on a sharp drop of the dollar’s rate in Russia. Referring
to the opinion of bankers and specialists, he tries to give
an answer to rank-and-file citizens to a question of inter-
est to anybody: is it worthwhile changing “greenbacks"
for roubles? The author notes that the Russian Central
Bank is a poor adviser in this case.

Legislator Boris Fedorov criticizes some Russian politi-
cians in the same newspaper who got accustomed to
refer to foreign economic experts but who are utterly
unable to apply their ideas to Russian actualities of life.

Churkin Briefs Press on Meeting With Milosevic

LD2006142895 Moscow ITAR-TASS in English
1352 GMT 20 Jun 95

[By Ivan Ivanov]

[FBIS Transcribed Text] BELGRADE June 20 (ITAR-
TASS) — Vitaliy Churkin, Russia’s Ambassador to
Belgium and NATO, had another meeting with Serb
President Slobodan Milosevic today. They discussed
ways of settling the Yugoslav crisis. The Russian
diplomat, who arrived in Belgrade on Monday, until
recently represented Russia at the negotiating process
on the settlement of the crisis in the Balkan Peninsula.
On Monday night, after the first meeting with Milosevic,
he left for Pale, where he held consultations vith leaders
of Bosnian Serbs.

On the basis of the results of those talks, he told Russian
journalists today, responding to a question of ITAR-
TASS, that he had arrived to Yugoslavia as a "semi-
official person” with the aim to hold "semi-official talks,
hoping to find a way out.” "I think, the trip was useful.
I heard a lot of interesting things from the people I
talked to, and I made some interesting observations. I
shall report the results of the talks to Moscow today,
and it is for them to decide what kind of policy Russia
will pursue in the Balkan Peninsula in the future,” he
said.

According to Churkin, his visits to Belgrade and Pale are
the result of Moscow’s concern over the current slicing
down of the conflicting parties to military methods of
the settlement of the crisis. This is why all ways and
means are being used for making them give priority to
political methods of achieving settlement.

Local diplomatic and political circles believe that in
this case Russia may come up with proposals on
changing the plan of the "contact group”, including the
partial changing of maps of the territorial division of
Bosnia and Herzegovina between Serbs and the Muslim
Croatian Federation, suggested by the international
mediators, and on granting the right to establish federal
relations with Yugoslavia to the Bosnian Serb Republic.

the resolution envisaging sending multinational instant
reaction forces to Bosnia.

We, he said, approve in principle the main orientation
of this resolution but “taking into consideration a
number of circumstances", abstained from voting for
this document.

The clauses of the resolution “create impression" and
the quick reaction forces will be directed against only
one of the Bosnian sides, the Russian diplomat pointed
out explaining the “circumstances”.

"Such an approach is incorrect as condemning actions
of Bosnian Serbs, we cannot but note at the same time
that the government of Bosnia-Herzegovina also bears
responsibility for provocations, violations of agreements
and direct attacks on United Nations Protection Forces
(UNPROFOR),” the official representative of the Rus-
sian Foreign ministry indicated.

The U.N. resolution may also give a “wrong signal” that
the UNPROFOR from the peacekeeping force is turning
into the force for coercing into peace, Mikhail Demurin
said.

Our concern about this comes from Russia’s position
on the settlement of the conflict in former Yugoslavia
which implies a political but not a forceful solution to
the problem, he stressed.

"The financial aspect of the activities of instant reaction
forces was not clear to us either," the Russian diplomat
noted.

Moscow will watch closely to ensure that the U.N. Pro-
tection Forces retain an unbiased and peacemaking char-
acter and their use for the protection of the U.N. staff or
in crisis situations be carried out “with maximum cir-
cumspection and maximum responsibility for possible
political and military consequences", Mikhail Demurin
emphasized.

of the Bosnia crisis to continue its efforts to ensure an
unbiased reaction of the U.N. Security Council and the
UNPROFOR [UN Protection Force] top command to
the situation emerged in the region."

On Russian Ambassador in Belgium and former Russian
Envoy for former Yugoslavia Vitaliy Churkin’s visit to
Belgrade, the spokesman said Russia was seeking to use
whatever chance there was for a Bosnian peace.

Demurin said regional tension was running an all-time
high, which demanded the urgent use of all channels of
influence available.

{[FBIS Transcribed Text] MOSCOW, June 20 (INTER-
FAX) — The crucial precondition of the success of the
Halifax summit which “proved respectable, rich in con-

in its preparation and holding, a Russian Foreign Min-

special services.”

both produce a program of specific activities in the field
and strengthen the G8 itself.

is an internal matter for Russia which must be resolved
by political means within the framework of the Russian

The danger posed by terrorists of any kind increases
a hundred times over if they possess means of mass
destruction. And the fact that this threat is real is
evidenced by the actions of the Japanese Aum Shirinkyo
religious sect, the frequent attempts to sell radioactive
materials needed to produce nuclear weapons, and other
facts. In this connection the participants in the Halifax
summit considered it necessary to accept Russia's
proposal and hold an unscheduled meeting devoted to
nuclear security problems, to which they will invite
the leaders of other states. It is noteworthy that this
conference will be held in Moscow. A fact which
experts assess as evidence of the growth of Russia’s
role in solving global problems.

It should certainly be stressed that in this respect the
Halifax summit marked a breakthrough for Russia.
As is known, these meetings of the leaders of the
world’s leading countries have been going on for 20
years. Russia has also participated in the last four.
And its involvement in the work of this “select club"
is increasing from year to year. Commenting on the
results of the summit which has just ended and the role
played by the Russian delegation, Sergey Medvedev,
the Russian Federation president’s press secretary, noted
that Halifax saw a reappraisal of Russia’s significance
in the international arena. Its voice is now heeded, and
it is taken into account when decisions are being made.

And you do not have to look far for examples. There

U.S.-PRC Rift ‘Not Necessarily’ Good for Moscow

MM2106091195 Moscow IZVESTIYA in Russian
20 Jun 95 p 3

[Report by Viadimir Skosyrev: "Moscow and Beijing:
Friends In Spite of Themselves"]

[FBIS Translated Text] Beijing — At the same time
as ties between Moscow and Beijing are developing
successfully — witness the visit by Prime Minister Li
Peng to Russia which begins this week — a serious
crisis has arisen in U.S.-Chinese relations. Just seven or

Budennovsk Hostage Drama Shifts to Chechen
Territory

Kulikov Refuses Safe Passage

LD2006121195 Moscow ITAR-TASS in English
1156 GMT 20 Jun 95

[By Gadzhi Shamov]

[FBIS Transcribed Text) MAKHCHKALA June 20
(ITAR-TASS) - Russian military commander in Chech-
nya Anatoliy Kulikov refused to meet the demand of
Chechen terrorist leader Shamil Basayev to issue written
guarantees for the safe passage of the terrorist convoy
across Chechnya and Basayev is now demanding the
guarantees from Prime Minister Viktor Chernomyrdin
or Interior minister Viktor Yerin.

The convoy of Chechen terrorists and Russian volunteer
prisoners who agreed to act as a "live shield’ for the
militants on their way from Budennovsk to Chechnya
remained in the Dagestani city of Khasavyurt on the
Chechen border as for 15.00 hours Moscow time (11.00
GMT).

Kulikov refused to sign any guarantees saying he “obeys
orders only from his superiors", and not from the ter-
rorists, according to the Dagestani Interior department.

The convoy of seven buses and a refrigerator truck with
dead terrorists is staying at the exit from Khasavyurt and
everything is quiet around it. 73 terrorists and some 120
hostages are in the buses, according to the Daghestani
Interior department.

The terrorists allow their voluntary prisoners to leave
the buses to stretch their legs, it said.

Although the situation looks quiet the terrorists and their
prisoners seem to be rather tense.

It is probable that the convoy would not cross into
Chechnya right at Khasavyurt, but would drive into the
mountains of the Novolak region of Dagestan. This will
make it cross another 100 kilometres to reach Chechnya.

At present the convoy is surrounded by three rings
of armoured vehicles, cars, army troops and police.
Combat helicopters permanently fly over the convoy.

It is expected that the voluntary hostages will be freed
in Zandak village of the Vedeno region of Chechnya,
according to Russian negotiator in Groz y Arkady
Volsky. The final place of destination of the terrorists is
expected to be the village of Dargo 10 kilometres east
of Vedeno.

‘Militants’ Identified, Warrants Issued

LD2006141395 Moscow INTERFAX in English
1221 GMT 20 Jun 95

[FBIS Transcribed Text] MOSCOW, June 20 (INTER-
FAX) — Six of the Chechen militants who staged the
terrorist attack in Budennovsk have already been identi-
fied and warrants for their arrest have been issued. The
warrant for the arrest of Shamil Basayev was issued ear-
lier under the article on banditism, Deputy Prosecutor
General of Stavropol Territory Aleksey Selyukov told
INTERFAX on Tuesday.

According to him, 40 investigators from the Stavropol
Territory Prosecutor’s Office and 20 investigators from
the local police are involved in investigating the crimi-
nal case connected with the events in Budennovsk. Over
300 witnesses have already been questioned, Selyukov
said. This work is led and coordinated by a group of of-
ficials from the Office of Russia’s Prosecutor General,
led by Deputy Prosecutor General Oleg Gaydanov.

A criminal case connected with the taking of hostages
by Chechen terrorists in Budennovsk was instituted
by the Stavropol Territory Prosecutor’s Office last
Wednesday under five articles of Russia’s Criminal
