["Press Review" — ITAR-TASS headline]

(FBIS Transciibed Text) MOSCOW, August 10 (Itar-
Tass) —- The Saturday focus of the Russian press is on
the August 9 inauguration ceremony of President Boris
Yeltsin for the second term.

Newspapers report Yeltsin's decree on resignation of the
government and nomination of Viktor Chernomyrdin for
the second term of premiership.

The State Duma, or the lower house of parliament,
meets today for an extraordinary meeting to approve
or reject Chernomyrdin in a secret vote.

Nezavisimaya Gazeta says “estimates suggest that the
vote required for the approval of the premier is in
principle available”.

The Komsomolskaya Pravda daily predicts a heated
debate over Chernomyrdin, but also a final majority
of votes for him. Most of the Duma’s pro-communist
faction and, understandably, the Our Home is Russia
faction, of which Chernomyrdin is the parent, are
thought to make the majority.

Chechnya remains in the spotlight.

“Chechen seperatists are controlling Groznyy. The Rus-
sian leadership looks apathetic and helpless,” the Segod-
nya newspaper says.

“On the day of swearing in the Russian president-elect,
the Chechen separatists have seized several governmen-
tal buildings in the Chechen republic,” reads the head-
line of Nezavisimaya Gazeta.

Trud daily.

"In the layered cake of Groznyy, in bloody street

“The best are being exterminated — soldiers, officers,

agreed on supplies of Ukrainian coal to Moldova

relations.

Transdniestr wader Igor Smirnov to Kiev, scheduled

discussed settlement of the Transdniestr situation.

convenient for Kuchma.

in Europe (OSCE), head of Switzerland's Federal De-

In a statement circulated in Bern, Cotti stressed thai

to assist the parties in the conflict to make it easier
to achieve the ceasefire and resume negotiations. Cotti
expressed profound concern over a new flare-up of
severe fighting in the Chechen capital. He believes
these military actions mean a breach of obligations
by the parties under the Moscow and Nazran accords
reached two months ago and make civilian population
in Groznyy suffer.

Cotti condemned the use of force in couflicts. He
believes the present violence and its consequences
jeopardise peace process in Chechnya. The OSCE head
urged the warring sides to cease fire to save lives
and resume the disrupted negotiations. He expects the
conflicting sides to give guarantees of immunity and
security to representatives of OSCE in Groznyy.

pendent state.

in conversation with your correspondent.

The Foreign Ministry disagrees categorically with that

perialism’ was invented by certain Ukrainian journal-
ists. I think Kiev should be satisfied with the arrival of
a diplomat with such an impressive track record.” And,
let us add on our own account, with such a unique rank.

Russia: Russia ‘Will Manage’ Without Conditional
IMF Loan

MM1208131696 Moscow RABOCHA?/A TRIBUNA
in Russian 10 Aug 96 p2

{Commentary by Aleksey Myasnikov under the "View
of the Situation" rubric: "We Need Money. But We
Need Partners Even More”"]

(FBIS Translated Text] Granting a loan or not is, of
course, an economic matter. There would be no need
to make that point were it not the IMF on the one side
and Russia on the other. Our country asked for the loan.
Everything seemed to have been agreed when the IMF
suddenly struck a pose, asking: How is the tax situatior
in your country”

“The IMF will postpone the allocation of the next
tranche of the $330-million loan until the situation
regarding tax receipts in the Russian Federation is
clarified," THE NEW YORK TIMES announced, citing
che IMF and the U.S. Administration. As is well known,
the IMF is under the complete control of the U.S.
Administration.

What does America want from us? Does it want to
ensure that everything is in order in our country? Well,
we want that ourselves. Among other things we already
have a draft Tax Code. Passions are running high, work
is in progress. Whether it is good or bad is Russia's
internal affair.

Stanley Fisher, deputy executive director of the IMF,
states: "We will probably postpone the allocation of the
tranche and then try to make up for lost time. I do not
think that we will miss the deadline if everything goes
according to plan.”

Fisher is trying to pull a fast one. Everyone knows
that the IMF is just as interested in giving loans to
countries like Russia as those countries have in seeking
them. Consequently the “benefactor’s” condescending
tone seems not quite appropriate here.

There is no denying that the country is in need. Not
of credits but of investments. What it needs is not
