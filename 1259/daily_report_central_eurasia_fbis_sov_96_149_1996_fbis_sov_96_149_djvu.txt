ready.

real danger, considering that the Federation Council is
invested with the right to impeach the president.

How Are Things for Them?

The United States has manned its Army on a voluntary
basis since 1973 — the decision was adopted in
connection with the need to enhance the prestige of
military service, which had fallen sharply owing to the
unpopularity of the war in Vietnam. Britain had gone
over to the professional principle even earlier, in 1961.
The armed forces of Canada, the Netherlands, Japan,
and certain other countries are similarly manned. France
is abandoning compulsory military service as of 1997.

What are the arguments in favor of such a manpower
acquisition system? Above all, the young citizens of a
country themselves choose military service, and this has
a positive effect on the atmosphere in the barracks and
the subunit. The reasons for such a choice can vary,
ranging from awareness of duty to the state to perfectly
pragmatic reasons. Hired servicemen, otherwise known
as contract servicemen, receive a wage which is signif-
icantly higher and, most importantly, more stable than
in civilian life. They are provided with free housing or

been thought out: In the army it is possible to obtain
a specialty, and the army will pay for postcontract ed-
ucation. Long service — contracts in the U.S. Army
are concluded for a period of three to six years with
a possible subsequent extension for a further six years,
and in the British Army for between six months and 22
years — naturally entails an increased level of profes-
sionalism. Thanks to this, a professional army appears
to many a well-adjusted mechanism.

No Such Thing as Soldiers for Free

But the truth, as always, lies somewhere in the middle.
The level of combat readiness of a professional army
is undoubtedly high. But it is no higher than in those
armies that are manned according to the principle of
compulsory service. The NATO Command, for exam-
ple, has repeatedly noted the training of Bundeswehr
soldiers, holding them up as an example to other ser-
vicemen, including U.S. ones. The Soviet Army never
played second fiddle to NATO either.

The explanation for this can be found in a number
of factors: traditions, national character.... And yet the
chief thing is the quality of army funding. If an army
receives sufficient funds to buy equipment and weapons
and for combat training, then the combat potential,
regardless of the principle of manpower acquisition, is
high. Conversely, poor funding for any army leads to
its breakdown.

administration made to the military budget during the

tankmen are required to cover, and so on. The legislators
did not fail to speak out on this. "The combat readiness
and combat ability of the armed forces are at a high
level at the present time, but cracks are already starting
to appear,” Arizona Senator John McCain declared.

substantially expanded.

Lithuania: Border Talks With Russia Inconciusive

WS0108083296 Tallinn BNS in English
1600 GMT 31 Jul 96

{FBIS Transcribed Text) Vilnius, Jul 31, BNS —
No significant progress was achieved in the round of
border between Russia and Lithuania which
ended Wednesday in Vilnius. “My optimism was 100
percent unfounded,” foreign ministry secretary Rimantas
Sidlauskas, head of the Lithuanian delegation, said at a
short briefing after the negotiations.

He noted that Lithuania had hoped for “decisive resolu-
tions” from Russia after a longer pause in negotiations
before the 15th round.

Despite this, the head of the Lithuanian negotiators
hopes that before the end of the year it will be

to resolve the most disputed issues. A possible
alternative now might be a meeting at the highest level
of officials, the foreign ministry secretary noted, but he
did not specify which officials he had in mind.

The previous round of talks took piace before the

of Lithuanian and Russian negotiations is not stalled
but there are no significant achievements, either. In

the Lithuanian negotiators had their first
meeting with the new head of the Russian negotiating
team, Ambassador Valeriy Popov. He replaced the
former head of the Russian delegation, Ambassador
Yuriy Shelmov.

The positions of both countries are getting closer,
yet there remain unsolved questions, said Zenonas
Kumetaitis, who is also deputy director at the State
Land Survey and Geodesy Service. According to him,
the Lithuanian and Russian delegations have different
interpretations of land border delimitation principles
and of international conventions, which govern the
delimitation of the economic zone and continental shelf
in the Baltic Sea.

During the negotiations, which have been going on since
July 1993, as much as 90 percent of the treaty on
land border has been agreed upon, however, there are
disagreements on the border along Lake Vistytis and the
mouth of the Nemunas.
