Clinton, Yeltsin Sign Strategic Agreement

LD3009105694 Moscow ITAR-TASS World Service in
Russian 2048 GMT 29 Sep 94

[By ITAR-TASS correspondent Mikhail Mzareulov]

{[FBIS Translated Text] Washington, 29 Sep—Russia
and the United States have achieved great progress in
enhancing global strategic stability and nuclear safety
since the end of the “cold war’. This was said in a “Joint
Statement on Strategic Stability and Nuclear Safety”
which was published on Thursday and signed here on
Wednesday by Presidents Boris Yeltsin and Bill Clinton.

The document notes that Russia and the United States
are continuing “‘to make significant reductions in their
nuclear forces.” The two countries’ reaiming of their
strategic missiles is also an important step.

Boris Yeltsin and Bill Clinton stressed that the United
States and Russia will cooperate with all the world’s
countries to achieve a successful end to the conference
on the Nuclear Nonproliferation Treaty in 1995 and the
“indefinite and unconditional” extension of the treaty.
The leaders of the two countries expressed their desire to
conclude a treaty on a universal banning of nuclear tests
during the very near future and to introduce a global ban
on the production of weapons-grade nuclear material.
They concluded that the permanent members of the UN
Security Council bear particular responsibility in the
nonproliferation of weapons of mass destruction. More-
over, the presidents stressed the importance of extending
the moratorium on nuclear explosions which is operating
in both countries.

The leaders of Russia and the United States agreed that
their countries’ defense ministers every three months
will exchange information on the strategic systems which
are due to be dismantled according to the START-I
treaty and on liow they are being liquidated. At the same
time Boris Yeltsin and Bill Clinton reaffirmed their
intention to seek an early ratification of the START-II
treaty and expressed the desire to exchange ratification
documents on this agreement during the next Russian-
U.S. summit. The presidents also decided that immedi-
ately after its ratification the United States and Russia
will begin dismantling al! strategic delivery systems
which are subject to reduction.

As the joint statement notes, the presidents of the two
countries agreed to step up the dialogue between the
United States and the Russian Federation with the
object of making a comparison of conceptual approaches
to the assignment of nuclear forces and to work out
specific steps towards adjusting them to the changing
world situation. They also discussed the possibilities of
adopting new confidence-building measures in this
sphere. After President Clinton had spoken during the
course of the talks about unilateral measures recently
taken in the United States to alter the composition of
nuclear forces and the approach to their use, Boris
Yeltsin stressed that similar steps are being introduced in

Russia with the aim of achieving a reduction in Russia’s
nuclear forces and enhancing their security. The leaders
of the two countries agreed that each side will examine
“further unilateral steps” to reduce the stockpiles of
these nuclear weapons.

Emphasizing the fundamental importance of main-
taining the ABM treaty in force, Boris Yeltsin and Bill
Clinton agreed to hold joint exercises of the ABM forces
[words indistinct] the theater of military operations and
early warning of missile launching. The Russian and
U.S. presidents see such exercises as “‘a basis for the joint
actions of U.S. and Russian forces, for example, during

The leaders of the two countries agreed “‘to act jointly in

ensuring nuclear security”. Boris Yeltsin and Bill
Clinton decided in particular to exchange information to
intercept illegal trade in radioactive materials, to
strengthen monitoring and physical guarding of such
substances, and regularly to exchange data on the overall
number of nuclear warheads, stocks of fissionable mate-
rials and their safe and secure storage. The presidents
decided that such forms of cooperation must develop
between government institutions responsible for keeping
track of, monitoring and guarding these materials,
defense ministries and scientific centers. Boris Yeltsin
and Bill Clinton reached agreement regarding the need
to implement a joint plan to build a new facility in
Russia for long-term storage of radioactive materials
from dismantled nuclear weapons.

Proceeding from the need to control other types of
weapons of mass destruction, the presidents of Russia
and the United States agreed to continue to observe the
terms of the U.S.-Russian-British 1992 declaration on

also in the need to have all countries of the world adhere
to the convention on the banning and dismantling of
chemical weapons and to give its terms a universal
nature.

Crimea on the grounds that, under specific conditions,

person could produce negative results. Alas, the events
that followed the election of president Yu.A. Meshkov
have confirmed the validity of such fears.

legislative authority, was consistently mounting guard
over the law, the president was given to violations of it,
explaining this by considerations of “expediency.” Yu.A.
Meshkov constantly demanded that the parliament grant

need for confirmation of the structure of the govern-
ment, this was not done for almost six months. More, the
Government of Crimea has not in eight months been
formed at all. Seven persons have been appointed to
ministerial office, but the status of the remaining 20 who
have been working with it is not known.

When, however, it became clear that many of the prom-
ises that the president had made in the period of the
election campaign were, to put it mildly, far from real-
ization, a search for “enemies” began in order to divert
Crimeans’ attention away from economic and other

[FBIS Translated Excerpt] One way of saving the eco-
nomically enfeebled Georgia is through the implementa-

We put several questions to Viadimer Papava, Georgian
minister of the economy.

Vladimer Giorgis dze [son of Giorgi] Papava was born in
1955 in Tbilisi into a famous family of economists. His
grandfather, Associate Professor Vladimer Papava, was
one of the founders of statistical sciences while his
father, Professor Giorgi Papava, is a well-known spe-
cialist in industrial economics. The sphere of research of
Vladimer himself—a doctor of economic sciences, pro-
fessor, director of the Georgian Academy of Sciences
Paata Gugushvili Economic Institute—is macroeconom-
ics.

[passage omitted]

[Papava] The main thing that has to be done urgently
today is to eradicate the distortions existing in the
Georgian economy. There is general debate concerning
the right direction in which to pursue economic
reforms—the neo-Keynesian or the neoconservative. |
believe such a debate is pointless when it is a matter of
the Georgian economy. Our most important and vital
task today is to revive the Georgian economy and,
therefore, all measures must be oriented solely to this
aim.

What distortions characterize the Georgian economy?
They are: First, Georgia’s monetary system is practically
in a total state of disarray. What is happening in Georgia
regarding the coupon is a farcical situation. It is not
excluded that, in the future, Georgia will enter the
literature on economic reforms as an extremely negative
example of what the state should not do regarding the
monetary system. All levels of power are guilty for the
coupon being unable to fulfill the role of money. Today,
the main thing is to create a monetary system.

The second distortion is the banking system. People do
not have faith in the banking system. It is essential to
reform this system: In order to get an entrepreneur or
individual citizen to put his trust in banks, it is essential
to auction off [auktsionireba] state commercial banks,
which has already started, and to remove all manner of
restrictions upon the movement of cash.

The third distortion: Our budget has an extremely large
deficit. It is essential to destroy the sources of the budget
deficit: Today, in order to cover this deficit, the state is
issuing money, which is the basis of inflation and is
radically undermining the economy.

It would take us a long time to list all the distortions but
the three that have been mentioned have to be eradi-
cated at once.

[Grigolia] Have the economic reforms really begun and, if
not, why not?

CAUCASUS 59

[Papava] Reforms are effectively under way. For
example, in May, the head of state signed an edict on
accelerating privatization, which is an almost revolu-
tionary document as far as privatization is concerned.
The second document (adopted at the end of August)
concerns the qualitative transformation of social protec-
tion, price formation, and the financial system. These
two documents determine the fundamental line that our
reforms are to take and so one can boldly say that
economic reforms in Georgia have begun. [passage
omitted]

[Grigolia] Does today’s reality in the economy give you
any grounds for optimism? First and foremost, what
contradictions do we need to overcome?

[Papava] On the one hand, we have grounds for opti-

optimism from the point of view that the head of state’s
two aforementioned edicts determine the qualitative
directions of economic reforms, that is to say, we are on
the right path. Such a government decision that either
partially or totally runs counter to the fundamental line
of economic reforms gives grounds for pessimism. | may
be mistaken, but today I view the implementation of the
economic reforms with more optimism and, therefore, I
am not thinking of resigning as minister. If a mood of
pessimism takes hold, there will be no sense in staying on
in this post.

[Grigolia] What real ways do you see of strengthening the

[Papava] As I told you, the main thing for us is to create

the coupon. Proceeding from this, it is essential that we
overcome those three distortions in our economy that we
mentioned earlier. Only following such restorative mea-
sures will it be expedient to introduce the lari. We have
taken steps in this direction and I hope that, in the next
few months, we will have tangible and real results. In
particular, these steps are: Firct, eradicating the sources
of the budget deficit and thereby stemming inflation.
With this aim, natural gas and electricity prices have
already risen, which should ensure that the cost of
importing or prow ucing them is completely covered and
the appropriate organizations receive a 10-percent
profit; second, at this stage, the question of regulating the
price of bread and travel on the subway is being partially
resolved; third, we have started reorganizing the banking
system—state commercial banks are being transformed
into joint-stock companies [aktsionireba] while, on the
other hand, a situation is being created that should
ensure that the restrictions on the movement of cash are
done away with. The social protection of the population
is necessary to achieve all this. We are trying to increase
wages and pensions proceeding from our maximum
economic potential today. As the situation improves, we
