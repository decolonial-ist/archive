[“Press Review” — ITAR-TASS headline}

{[FBIS Transcribed Text) MOSCOW July 16 (lItar-
Tass) — Follows a review of Tuesday's Moscow-based
Russian newspapers dated July 16.

ROSSIYSKAYA GAZETA on Russian President's
post-election Decrees aimed at strengthening public
security.

The newspaper refers to the Decrees as a “salvo of
Presidential Decrees against terrorism, corruption and
crime”. One of the Decrees deals with “urgent measures
to strengthen law and order aad step up action against
crime in Moscow City and Region". The newspaper also
runs a Decree and Regulations for a Security Council
of the Russian Federation.

ROSSIYSKAYA GAZETA on pensions system.

The newspaper's readers ask whether a Pensions Fund
will exist or not. The overall level of the provision of
pensions has impermissibly failen of late, and delays in
the payment of pensions, for periods of up to 30 days at
times, have become a usual thing. Experts consider that
only the adoption of a law “On State Pension Insurance
in the Russian Federation" can change the situation.

ROSSIYSKAYA GAZETA again returns to the re-
cent trolleybus blasts in Moscow. Analyst Vladimir
Yanchenkov makes an attempt at a journalistic inquiry
into blasts-related leads.

KRASNAYA ZVEZDA on the same subject.

“Whoever was the organiser of the brazen and barbarous
challenge to the authorities — an ungovernable Chechen
militant or a new Russian mafioso — he should not be
allowed to triumph in the final analysis", the newspaper
points out.

ROSSIYSKIYE VESTI on the Constitutional Court
decision on the “CPSU case”, four years after the event.

The newspaper's correspondent Dmitriy Orlov inter-
viewed Tamara Morshchakova, Deputy Head of the
Constitutional Court of the Russian Federation, asking
her opinion as to how the ruling by the Constitutional
Court, which made in point of fact a compromise deci-
sion on the notorious “Communist Party of the Soviet
Union (CPSU) case", can be evaluated now, four years
after the start of the trial. The newspaper runs the in-
terview under the headline “The decision on the ‘CPSU
case’ is being violated by the Communist Party and is
not implemented by bureaucrats”.

INTERREGIONAL AFFAIRS 1

KABOCHAYA TRIBUNA carries an article by Yelona
Yegiozarova about the price of wearing fine clothes.

What should be done for the desire of the “average
statistical Russian woman” to look fashionable would
not result in a family budget gap, the writer of the article
asks, and suggests as one of the ways out as follows: “it
is possible to buy Ivanovo-produced cloth and German
fashion plates and sew a dress on one’s own".

KOMSOMOLSKAYA PRAVDA on remarks by Gen-
eral Lev Rokhlin, member of the State Duma, exposing
corruption in the army.

The newspaper's correspondent Viktor Sokirko, in an
article illustrated with photo pictures, recalls that “those

named in General Rokhlin’s report were Defence Min-
istry’s top brass, beginning from former Defence Minis-
ter Pavel Grachev. The generals, anticipating an adverse
turn of events, hurried to the countryside urgenty to fi-
nalise the construction of their dachas (country houses)”.

{FBIS Transcribed Text) Riga, Jul 12, BNS — The navy
exercise Amber Sea is aimed at preparing ships to form
a common Baltic squadron, Latvian Navy Cmdr. Gaidis
Zeibots told reporters Friday.

The training requires a close cooperation of the Baltic
navies and it will be useful for the joint work in the

squadron, he said.
Amber Sea is the so far largest common exercise of the

Baltic naval forces and will involve 14 war ships: eight

The exercise will take place July 12-22 in the Guif of
Riga, Gulf of Finland id Estonian territorial waters.

The first Amber Sea exercise took place in the Latvian
territorial waters in May 1995 but the number of

participating ships was smaller.
The first stage of this year’s exercise will proceed July

12-16 in Riga. It will include planning and visits to
places of interest in the Latvian capital.

The first practical part of the training will take place
July 16-19 and focus on crossing of mine fields, mine
sweeping, rescue operations and maneuvers.

The second stage is scheduled for July 19-21 in the Gulf
of Finland to train in shooting and embargo operations.

The exercise will wind up July 21 and 22 in Tallinn.

Latvia will be represented to the exercise by mine
trawlers Imanta and Viesturs, coastal guard vessels
Kometa, Klints, Kristaps and Gaisma, rescue ship Mara
and cutter KA-11, as well as an air force helicopter.

Estonian ships will include the mine ship Sulev, patrol
boat Ahti and two border guard vessels — Pikker and
Kou.

The Lithuanian navy will sent the frigate Zemaitis and
patrol boat Vetra to the exercise.

After the training, Baltic navy commanders will take a
unit of Baltic ships, including Sulev, Imanta, Zemaitis
and Vetra, on pay an unofficial visit to Helsinki.

{Report by Aleksandr Yerastov: “Gore Is Not Far Off
— He Is With Chernomyrdin”]

{FBIS Translated Excerpt] U.S. Vice President Albert
Gore arrived in Moscow Saturday: He is taking part in
the session of the commission which, in particular, also
bears his name, which opened Monday. The point is that
for the sake of brevity the Russian-U.S. commission
on economic and technological cooperation is named
for its co-chairmen — Russian Federation Government
head Viktor Chernomyrdin and Albert Gore — the U.S.
Administration's number two.

The Chernomyrdin-Gore commission was created in
accordance with the Vancouver declaration adopted on
the basis of the meeting between the U.S. and Russian
Federation presidents in April 1993. [passage omitted]

On the agenda of the present, seventh, session of
the commission which was discussed Monday during
a meeting at the government house by Viktor Cher-
nomyrdin and Albert Gore are questions of trade and
investment cooperation, energy and the conversion of
biological weapons, the peaceful use of nuclear energy,
and mutual access to each other’s markets. As at pre-
vious sessions there is a discussion of collaboration in
space research, in the fight against crime, in the com-
mercial sphere, in the solution of problems of the non-
fulfillment of financial obligations, and the improvement
of the taxation system, and also in the protection of in-
tellectual property.

Within the framework of cooperation between the Rus-
sian fuel and energy complex and its U.S. counterpart
it was proposed to step up control over the “movement”
of radioactive substances and also to study joint activ-
ity in the elaboration of Arctic oil and gas deposits and
to enhance the reliability and safety of nuclear electric
power stations.

The session, which ends Tuesday, will not bypass
a single one of the sharp corners of economic and
technological cooperation between the United States and
Russia. Thus it is expected that it will also touch on the
question of the incident connected with the export of the
notorious “Bush chicken legs” to our country. Actually,
agreement has already been reached here: The Russian
side has rejected a categorical embargo on transatlantic
chicken legs and the Americans in response have agreed
to take into account the “blacklist” which our side has
prepared of suppliers from the United States who in
Moscow's opinion have failed to observe the necessary
sanitary norms.

Russia: Gore, Chernomyrdin Discuss Baykal
Project

(FBIS Transcribed Text] MOSCOW, July 16 (Interfax)
— Co-chairmen of the Russian-U.S commission for
economic and technological cooperation Viktor Cher-
nomyrdin and Albert Gore studied a project of geo-
information system for Russia's Lake Baykal this morn-
ing.

The joint project, called Geo-Information System-
Baykal, aims to support new methods of environmental
protection and stable economic development of the
Baykal region with the use of digital cartography,
remote probing and geo-information systems.

Project heads informed the Russian prime minister and
the U.S. vice president of the system's possibility to
analyze land uses in the delta of the Selenga River,
which falls into Lake Baykal.

The project became possible thanks to the exchange of
satellite maps, which used to be classified for national
security reasons.

The project will ensure Russian and U.S. security in-
terests and enable exchange of economic and environ-
mental information. Chernomyrdin and Gore praised the
work of project participants and said they hope for the
project’s success.

"It would be good to hold a regular meeting of the
commission next summer in the Baykal area,” said
Chernomyrdin. Gore fully supported this idea.

According to sources in the U.S. delegation, the
commission co- chairmen have left for Barvikha, the
Moscow region, where Russian President Boris Yeltsin
is expected to receive Albert Gore.

This afternoon the co-chairmen will attend a working

lunch with members of the Russian and U.S. business
communities at the Metropol hotel, downtown Moscow.
