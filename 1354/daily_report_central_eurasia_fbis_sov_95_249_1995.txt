Yabloko Bloc Criticizes Timing of Election Runoffs

LD2712125295 Moscow INTERFAX in English
1103 GMT 27 Dec 95

{FBIS Transcribed Text] MOSCOW, Dec 27 (Interfax)
— The Moscow electoral commission has violated
the existing legislation by slating the second round of

elections for December 29 and 30, or
the eve of New Year’s celebrations, the Russian Duma
faction of the YaBLoko movement said.

The statement was signed by YaBLoko leader Grigoriy
Yavlinskiy and transmitted Wednesday to Interfax. It
accuses the electoral commission of "a gross violation
of Art. 30 of the law on the basic guarantees for suffrage
in the Russian Federation."

The law demands that elections be held on one day,
that the day set for elections be a holiday, and that
the citizens be informed of the date and place of the
elections twenty days, and not a week, before the event,
the YaBLoko statement says.

The YaBLoko faction fears that public control of the
elections will be “highly problematic on the eve of the
celebrations.”

{FBIS Translated Text] The week’s break which the
"Russia Is Our Home” [NDR] movement executive
committee had taken to consider the results of the recent
State Duma elections ended at the “Lesnyye dali” rest

the NDR party lists and “single-seaters” attracted to this
movement gathered there for their first organizational
meeting. Together with the elaboration of the tactics of
the struggle for influence in the State Duma the debate
on the banks of the Moscow river also gives grounds for
supposing that the NDR and perhaps the party’s leader,
V. Chernomyrdin, intend to struggle for the presidential
seat in 1996.

This story looks entirely feasible in the light of a whole
series of facts. First, the president seems deliberately
to be in no haste to create his own pre-election
staff although no one in his entourage doubts that
B. Yeltsin will run. On Staraya Square only a small

Chernomyrdin at the “Lesnyye dali" meeting. “The
movement's executive committee and its headquarters

In this connection informed sources in the top echelons

There is no doubt that much will have to be done for
the presidential elections. Primarily it will be necessary
to rally around the NDR the reformist section of the
deputies’ corps and most importantly to break the
"leftism"” trend in society which emerged with the
Communists’ victory at the parliamentary elections. In
this connection we can consider V. Chernomyrdin’s
speech to his fellow fighters to be a policy-making
one. He did not dramatize the existing situation. "No
hopelessness and futility as a result of the elections!"
he stressed. The NDR as a movement of centrists,
reformists, and statesmen has every chance of attracting
deputies of democratic bent to its ranks and of defending
the reforms course. Nor should there be any doubt that
to push forward reformist draft laws and decisions the
faction will seek allies and partners not only in the
“right” wing of the State Duma but also in the “left”
wing. To be more precise, in the social-democratic
part of the Communists’ faction. "And there is nothing
seditioius in that,” V. Chernomyrdin said.

The premier did not go into the details of the plan
of actions the NDR is elaborating for the forthcoming
six months. He merely stressed that the results of
the elections attest to some blunders and setbacks for
the government. They cannot be ignored. Nonetheless
the course of reforms should remain as before but
amendments must be made to it without delay.

As a priority task V. Chernomyrdin cited restricting
the growth of prices to 1.5-2 percent a month so that
credits become cheaper and investments "go through."

Gaydar Warns of ‘Upheavals’ in Store
MM2712141795 Moscow IZVESTIYA in Russian
26 Dec 95 pS

{Interview with Yegor Gaydar, leader of “Russia's
Democratic Choice-United Democrats" electoral bloc,
by Nikolay Bodnaruk; place, date not given: “Yegor
Gaydar: This Is Merely the Prelude to New Upheavals
in Russia”)

(FBIS Translated Text) [Bodnaruk] What are your
dominant feelings, Yegor Timurovich? Annoyance with
yourself, resentment at voters...

[Gaydar] Good grief, what is the point in feeling
resentment at them? They are the only voters we have.

(Bodnaruk} But why has society suddenly rushed back
to communism?

[Gaydar] What has happened is what was bound to hap-
pen. There is in fact a logic in postcommunist transfor-
mations — it is now clearly visible. In simpie terms, it
looks as follows. First: The communist regime collapses
amid a crisis when everything that can be squandered
has been squandered. Two: The governments that come
in on a wave of naive anticommunist populism inherit
a ruined economy. Three: In these conditions economic
transformations always prove difficult everywhere, be-
cause from the very first day the government is faced
with a choice between very unpleasant decisions. Four:
Even where the transformations proceed successfully,
they throw up new problems along with new opportuni-
ties. For instance, a different form of inequality: Instead
of hidden privileges like Department 100 in the GUM
store and special distribution centers, there is an open,
glaring form of inequality that is unfamiliar to soci-
ety. All this, taken together, whips up a wave of social
protest that is vigorously exploited by postcommunist
parties. They often come to power on the crest of this
wave in the second elections after the start of reforms.
Then there is a parting of the ways. In East Europe
the vassal communist parties are indeed evolving in the
direction of social democracy, but in our country the
imperial communist party is evolving in the direction
of national-socialism. So there is a common problem,
but different degrees of risk.

[Bodnaruk] Today you can hear various assessments of
the election results, ranging from “nothing terrible" to
“a disaster.” How do you see this?

[Gaydar] I see it as a defeat for Russian democracy.
As the prelude to grave upheavals for our country and
as a serious risk of dangerous new experiments. There
is a rising tide. I know it will come. And I know it
will pass. It will be shorter than the last 70-year one

(FBIS Transcribed Text) Vilnius, Dec 27, BNS — The
crises at the two largest Lithuanian commercial banks
— the Lithuanian Joint-Stock Innovative Bank (LAIB)
and the Litimpeks Bank — are no cause for panic, an-
nounced the chiefs of the Lithuanian administration and
the Bank of Lithuania. They also categorically coun-
tered allegations that these two possible bankruptcies
cases could cause a chain reaction among Lithuanian
commercial banks.

Prime Minister Adolfas Slezevicius stated at a press
conference on Friday that Lithuanian banks have enough
liquidity to allow stability of the banking system to
be guaranteed. According to the premier, citizens with
savings in the country’s other commercial banks can rest
assured that their deposits are secure. No restrictions on
withdrawal of deposits are going to be introduced —
now or in the future.

The banking operations of LAIB and Litimpeks, on
the other hand, are unlikely to be resumed for another
“several months.” Slezevicius said that the two culprits
had to be halted to prevent embezzlement of money in
the accounts of these banks.

Kazys Ratkevicius, chairman of the board of the central
bank, said at the same press conference that there were
about 320 million litas in the accounts of Lithuania's
commercial banks at this point, and that in their safe
deposit boxes, as of Friday, another additional 35
million litas and 11 million USD were being held.

Ratkevicius also stated that the crises at LAIB and
Litimpeks would have absolutely no effect on the
stability of the Lithuanian currency. Standing behind
that stability were reserves of more than 800 million
USD, an awesome guarantee.
