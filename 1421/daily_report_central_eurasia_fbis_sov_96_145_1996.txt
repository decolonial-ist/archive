There exists such a concept as the strategy of indirect

a chosen aim without any “direct” acuion. It seems to

Let us take a close look at what is escaping the attention
of many analysts — the decision’s timing. Why was the
decision to enlarge NATO adopted in the second half
of last year (when this was mooted long ago)? Clearly
nothing out of the ordinary had happened in the West
The same could be said from 4 global perspective. So
had something, perhaps, happened in the East? In the
post-Soviet area?

warm.” An event had indeed occurred 1n the East whose
consequences might have impelied NATO to adopt
the difficult decision. But what was the situation in
question?

attention to one of those processes that was overshad-
owed by our troubles in the general consciousness. It 1s
the growth of the trend toward integration in the post-
Soviet area — a phenomenon which was particularly

lows: The European consortium controls 50-60 percent
of the market, the remainder is shared by the Ameri-
can corporations Lockheed Martin and McDonnel! Dou-
gias and by China. Their total share is 25-26 percent.
It would seem that Russia has quite good chances of
getting all the rest and subsequently even increasing its
share. But the market is not a bazaar and our “space
chariot” which once amazed the world with its rapid
advance has slowed down by the side of the road and
is quite unable to get back onto the highway. alas, that
is the case.

Why? Its former might has been lost (in previous
years we Carried out up to 100 launches a year) and
production has come to a halt, ideas are exhausted,
there is no money? All this is so to some degree or
other. Full financial dependence on state subsidies given
the present deficit in the tattered budget is of course
clipping the wings of new projects. All is not well in our
space industry and the scientific research institutes and
design bureaus which, incidentally, are world-famous
and world-recognized. And not everything is successful
as regards the ability to conduct commercial deals
(or simply not to allow ourselves to be swindled or
shortchanged).

Deceptive Simplicity or Solo Voyage

For some reason there is a current opinion that the
“scheme for prosperity” lies in the rapid and successfu!
sale of our developments abroad in order to grab a profit
as rapidly as possible, especially as our space industry
is a tasty morsel for our foreign “partners.” And they
are drawn to Russia's essentially unique know-how.
The “Energiya” space rocket corporation has managed
to invent a system for the rendezvous and docking of
spacecraft while the “Salyut" and “Khimmash” design
bureaus have invented technology for the production of
a cryogenic hydrogen-oxygen engine and the Gagarin

Of course it is hard to reject such deals using “moral”

I have absolutely no wish to say that we do not have
examples of considered and advantageous commerce.
A leading firm of ours, the Khrunichev State Space

successfully. The projects in which it takes part and its
mutual relations with foreign partners (the state center
has contracts for $1.5 billion) are the best evidence of
that. I should add that visits to the “Mir” orbital complex

These and other “receipts” can to some extent save
or support the Russian space industry under conditions
where the government is failing to observe the schedule
and volumes of payments to space industry enterprises.
Today this process is more reminiscent of an eye-
dropper than of the necessary injections. Yet every large
firm is linked with many contractors and must ensure
prompt prepayment for subassemblies and so forth. And
if this is lacking the space industry dies. Hence the talk
of the loss of scientific and technical potential and the
forfeiting of the status of a great space power.

Of course, participation in the “Alpha” project is pres-

own “Mir” has worked for 10 years and will remain in
space until the year 2000. That is entirely feasible. But
then? “Mir-2” has suffered the same sorry oblivion as
the long-suffering “Buran.” Is that prospect to Russia's
advantage? Is the “kindly uncle” selfless? A situation
could arise in which we are the "poor relations” in “Al-

Game Worth the Candle

Meanwhile we must penetrate the space market. There
is no alternative. First, it is super-profitable (experts
assess the cost of services at $1.5 billion a year)
and, second, establishment on this market and the
commercialization of national space programs provide a
guarantee of stability for our space firms. And not only
for them. In the West the return on space investments
is $3-15 for each dollar invested and as we know
space communications are five or six times cheaper
than cable or radio relay communications and orbital
reconnaissance of natural resources could ensure a
profit of 7:1 that is seven units of profit for each
unit of expenditure. And there is economic monitoring,
navigation, and cartography! It is not hard to calculate
that space technology can resolve many terrestrial
problems with great efficiency.

The question of why we are not making money out
of space is by no means rhetorical. The fact that on
the international space market there is very tough and
wily competition does not need to be explained. The
market is the market and it lives by its own complex
laws. But that is only one reason for our lack of
progress, slowness, and dilatoriness. It seems to me
that the main reason is that there are no real levers for
collaboration between state and commercial structures
in the use of space. And these levers will not appear
without a smoothly functioning legal basis for this
collaboration, without the creation in the country of a
considered market infrastructure capable of sensitively
taking its pulse and placing capital promptly and reliable
in priority programs and projects.

restrictions (or quotas as they call them) on our space
services to other countries. America is zealously pro-

under fear of tough sanctions.

{(FBIS Translated Text} It is becoming increasingly
obvious that the Federal Assembly, and particularly the
State Duma, is sharply polarized. It is naive to claim
that contradictions are only natural and are beneficial
to the process of lawmaking. The point is this: Will
the sound, state-patriotic forces be able to stabilize the
situation in the legislative organ, which should without
fail have a beneficial effect on the political situation
in the country, so as to give the state the opportunity
thereafter to take at least a little step forward, toward
development, without “great upheavals”? And therefore
indications of that kind were what was sought at the
latest, routine press conference given by State Duma
Chairman Gennadiy Nikolayevich Seleznev.

RUSSIA NATIONAL AFFAIRS

Russia Does Not Need a Second Parliament

{Correspondent} What were the results of the first,
spring session of the second State Duma?

{Seleznev] I am satisfied with the results of the first
session. One hundred and forty-five federal laws were
adopted and 76 were signed by the president, in
addition to which the Duma adopted 74 draft laws
at the first reading and seven at the second. Forty-
eight plenary sessions were held. And it must be taken
into account that the work was taking place against an
unusual political background — the two rounds of the
presidential elections. But we reached agreement with
the factions and not a single session was postponed
or disrupted; we provided the legislative basis for the
elections. There was another result too: The factions
and deputies’ groups formed at the very beginning of
the Duma’s work survived; the predictions that they
would start to fall apart after the elections proved to
be idle conjecture. The only thing we are not satisfied
with is the “loose ends." Laws remained unapproved
at the third reading; drafts were not given their second
and third readings. Of course, the committees would
have liked to adopt them as quickly as possible, and 70
questions were submitted to the last session. Naturally
there is not enough time, and some of the drafts will
have to be carried over until the fall. There is nothing
terrible about that, we will begin the fall session more
effectively because of this groundwork.

[Correspondent] How satisfied are you with your collab-
oration with the Federation Council? And what is the
relationship like with the Political Consultative Coun-
cil?

[Seleznev] We are satisfied with relauons with the
Federation Council. Although experience is already
suggesting that we chose the worst of the three drafts
of the law on the Federation Council. It turns out that
the heads of the legislative and executive branches of
power, who have plenty of worries in their region, are
having to tear themselves away from these concerns,
travel to Moscow, and hastily discuss and adopt laws.
For our part, we have already realized how difficult it
is to adopt a good law, and because of this, differences
arise among ourselves. We feel that the members of the
Federation Council do not even have time to study a
law properly, and basically adopt it on the say-so of
apparatus workers. Obviously thought will have to be
given to the question of how to form the upper chamber
more sensibly.

As for the new body that has been set up under the
president [the Political Consultative Council], I do not
yet know what its job will be. But I wish to say to both
the president and the deputies: Don't take parliament

{FBIS Translated Text] Arkhangelsk — The sufferings
of the “radioactive cows” from Mogilev in Arkhangelsk
lasted about a month. The central city market had been
supplied with the 17 tonnes of beef by a commercial or-
ganization. The very first tests carried out by specialists
in the radiology department of the veterinary laboratory
found strontium in the bones of the Belarusian cows
three times higher than the permitted concentration.

The level of cesium, which builds up in muscular tis-
sue, was found to be normal. In other words, the meat
was relatively clean, but the bones were “contaminated.”
The businessmen suggested that it was all pure in-
trigue on the part of the local “meat mafia": “This meat
is accepted everywhere in Russia, apart from here in
Arkhangelsk...." The beef was analyzed by another two
laboratories independent of each other: At the sanita-
tion and epidemiology center and the local university.
Strange though it may seem, the mafia was not involved
on this occasion. The results of the repeat tests were the
same: The bones contained three times the “normal”
amount of strontium.

“The test results are amazingly uniform,” a laboratory
worker said. “The whole consignment of meat is con-
taminated to approximately the same degree. This means
that the whole herd of cattle either grazed on "bad" pas-
ture or was given ‘contaminated’ feed....”

Will the incident lead to the breakup of the “meat friend-
ship” between Arkhangelsk Oblast and Belarus? The
authorities take an unequivocal view: It will not. Spe-
Cialists are not inclined to be prejudiced against Belaru-
sian beef. Several consignments of meat, including from
Mogilev Oblast, have very successfully passed tests by
veterinary and sanitation experts in Arkhangelsk and
been allowed onto the local market.

The incident is unprecedented in the oblast, but certainly
not in the country as a whole. A radiology expert, who
wished to remain anonymous, showed me a document
from Moscow signed by the chief state doctor responsi-
ble for sanitation. It begins with the rather telling words:
“In connection with the increasing frequency of contam-
inated agricultural produce being brought into Russian
territory...” and prescribes stricter controls over meat
originating in former Soviet republics.

In view of the political delicacy of the incident, efforts
were made to settle the matter without provoking an

RUSSIA NATIONAL AFFAIRS 21

international scandal. Publicity could not be avoided,
however. Obviously, the news that 17 tonnes of radioac-
tive beef had arrived in the city was not a cause for
celebration. Arkhangelsk (amongst other oblast centers)
has been fed on contaminated meat before — soon after
the disaster at Chernobyl. Precisely for this reason, no
one eager to remove the clean meat from the contam-
inated bones could be found. The rather limp scandal
ended with the beef being expelled from the oblast. All
17 tonnes of meat were loaded into a trailer a few days
ago, apparently bound for home. No one believes that
the beef will return to Mogilev, of course. The most
likely scenario is that it will be sold en route.

Incidentally, according to the documents held by its
owners, it is totally free of contamination.

enterprises cannot pay for it, and consequently cannot
swell the treasury coffers. Aleksandr Kosolapov, deputy
chief of the tax reforms department, told IZVESTIYA
that all sectors are complaining of excessive taxes. But
if they are all exempted from these taxes then they will
complain again that they are not being financed from
the budget.

According to the figures of the Ministry of Fuel and
Energy itself, taxes comprise 70-85 percent of the price
of their output.

The problem could be resolved in the interests of
Russian operators by the Duma’s adoption of a special
section of the tax code and a law on production sharing.
This could happen at the end of the Duma’s two August
sessions when the deputies will meet for the president's
inauguration.

Georgia: Foreign Minister Holds News Conference
on Visit to Iran

LD2407142796 Tbilisi IPRINDA in Russian
1310 GMT 24 Jul 96

{(FBIS Translated Excerpt] Tbilisi, 24 Jul — An
IPRINDA correspondent reports that today Georgian
Foreign Minister Iralkli Menagharashvili held a news
conference and summed up his visit to the Islamic
Republic of Iran.

The minister noted that the aim of the visit was to
develop Iranian-Georgian relations, to discuss a wide
range of issues concerning regional and international
relations, relations between Iran and Georgia on the
international arena, as well as Iran's stance on Georgia
and the Caucasian and Black Sea countries.

Discussions of economic relations occupied an impor-
tant part. In connection with Georgia’s debt to Iran,
which amounts to $12.5 million, the minister said an
agreement had been reached on the issue and the Geor-
gian delegation members who remained in Iran would
Sign an agreement on restructuring the debt.

Iran is keen to cooperate in rehabilitating Georgia's
power complex and in particular, in rehabilitating the
Zhinvali hydroelectric power station and to restore the
Iran-Armenia power line.

As regards the development of the infrastructure for
regional transport, Iran is keen to study transit routes
to Russia and Europe through the Caucasus, as well as
the transportation of Turkmen gas.

Georgia has agreed to be allocated a space on the Iranian
island of Kish and to develop it to participate in the
establishment of an economic zone there.

Georgia wishes to gain observer status in the Economic
Cooperation Organization, the headquarters of which are
in Tehran. [passage omitted]

A memorandum on cooperation between the Georgian
and Iranian commissions with UNESCO has been

signed.
