Integration

LD1908180596 Moscow INTERFAX in English
1727 GMT 19 Aug 96

{FBIS Transcribed Text] Moscow, Aug 19 (INTER-
FAX) — The next CIS summit will consider a long-
term plan for CIS integration and about a dozen other
draft documents in December 1996, according to its
draft agenda forwarded to Interfax Monday.

In particular, the agenda envisions a Cebate on several
documents on cooperation in the legal field.

The agenda mentions such documents as a protocol to
the Convention of Legal Assistance in Civil, Matrimo-
nial and Criminal Cases of January 22, 1993; treaty on
cooperation between CIS law enforcers; convertion of
mutual recognition and implementation of court deci-
sions on administrative and traffic violations; conven-
tion of extradition of the convicted for the service of
imprisonment terms; aed convention on extradition of
mentally ill people for obiigatory treatment.

The draft agenda also includes an agreement on the
supply of laid-off servicemen with housing in all CIS
states.

The military sections include such documents as a
declaration on the principles of guarding the external
CIS borders; additions to the regulations on the CIS
Council of Border Troops Commanders; a decision on
the introduction of special signs for CIS border troops
commanders; and a list of special cargo and military
products to be freely transported throughout the CIS.

The agenda also includes the presence of the CIS Col-
lective Peacekeeping Force in Abkhazia and, possibly,
amendments to its status.

The summit will also consider an agreement on the le-
gal status of state representatives and officials of the
CIS Interstate Economic Committee, urgent measures
to develop the intergovernmental TV and radio broad-
casting company Mir and activities to celebrate the fifth
anniversary of the CIS.

Twenty thousand is not such a great figure even for such
a small country as Moldova. But there are some specific
nuances.

Out of some 4.5 million citizens living in Moldova,
there were only about 2.4 million voters registered at the
1994 parliamentary elections and 1995 local elections.
Approximately 150,000 of them reside in Dniester.
The above-mentioned signatures are supposed to be
raised from not less than one-third of districts, cities
and towns. At calculating the signatures, only those
localities shall be considered valid where a minimum
of 1,000 signatures have been raised. If one takes into
consideration that each voter can sign only for one
candidate, then it becomes quite clear that the struggle
for signatures is going to be very tough.
Understanding this, candidates’ support teams began
traveling to various localities long before August 19,
demonstrating a vivid interest about resident lists in
housing offices and at local power bodies in villages.
And political circles have been spreading rumougg about
a possibility of appearance of commercial structures to
raise and trade the signatures, like the ones that emerged
in Russia only a few months ago.

At any rate, observers admit that the world-renowned
practice of signature raising is quite suitable for
Moldova as a method of filtering off reckless politi-
cians who are seeking to try their “strength” even in
situations when they realize their chances are next to
zero. In that sense, one can only regret that the minimal
number is only 20,000 signatures, not 100,000 or at
least 50 thousand, as was envisaged by the initial draft
of the election law.

One can state with confidence that the 20,000 barrier
will be easily overcome by the current president Mircea
Snegur, who has been actively using the well-developed
local structures of his Party of Revival and Accord
[PRCM], as well as the assistance of a lot of smaller
parties and movements backing him.

Approximately the same story is with Prime Minister
Andrei Sangheli [of the] Agrarian Democratic Party
of Moldova (PDAM). And although some regional
structures of the Agrarian Democratic Party have come
to support President Snegur or Parliament Chairman
Lucinschi, the ruling party is still strong enough to raise
the required number of signatures.

be registered as an independent candidate as well. He is
fairly popular in this republic, and enjoys sympathies of
a big part of Chisinau residents. Besides them, Lucinschi
