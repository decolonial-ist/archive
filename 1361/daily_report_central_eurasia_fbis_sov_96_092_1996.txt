as of April).

The group of World Bank experts will leave Vilnius on
May 10.

FBIS-SOV-96-092

The key issues discussed at talks between the Lithuanian
administration and the international bodies’ officials
were restructuring of banks, a cut in import taxes on
agricultural produce and a rise in energy prices.

The prime minister told reporters it was agreed to freeze
import taxes for the time being and to put off deadlines
for their reduction to a later date.

"We have to protect our agriculture,” Stankevicius said.

The IMF wants Lithuania to reduce import taxes on
major agricultural produce to 20 percent by September
this year. A further 10 percent cut must take place by
September of the next year.

Also, the IMF urges for a sharp increase in thermal
energy prices to consumers as early as this June.
The Lithuanian government, however, suggests that the
increase should take place from the start of the next
heating season at the earliest.

“Introduction of new rates is a subject to be negotiated
with the IMF because we have to weigh everything very
carefully,” Stankevicius told reporters.

A draft resolution worked out by the Energy Ministry
provides for a 40 percent increase in the heat energy
costs to consumers.

Though the IMF and World Bank missions are leaving
without any clear results, the two sides “are close to
each other,” Stankevicius said.

The premier said he did not think the cooperation
between Lithuania and the financial institutions would
break off.
