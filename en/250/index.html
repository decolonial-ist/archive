Campaigns

  • Truce of Andrusovo (1667)
    Hrushevsky records the reaction to the partition: "there was great resentment against the Muscovite government for agreeing to cede Right-Bank Ukraine to Poland in the armistice of 1667: it divided Ukraine with Poland and did not keep what it had promised when taking Ukraine under the tsar's hand"
  • Mazepa's Defection to Sweden and the Baturyn Massacre (1708)
    The massacre ran in parallel with propaganda: "Tsarist letters were circulated among the people claiming that Mazepa had gone over to the Swedes in order to hand Ukraine to Poland, to introduce the Union in Ukraine and eradicate the pious faith; that he was an apostate, a secret Catholic"
  • Military Liquidation of the Zaporozhian Sich (1775)
    The Zaporozhians were even faulted for their own husbandry: they "were establishing their own tillage and thereby severing their dependence on the Russian state"

Procedures

  • Truce of Andrusovo (1667) — technique Collusion with a Third Party Behind the People's Back
    "Muscovy then did not give it back"
  • Publication of the "Synopsis" (1674) — technique Denial of a Distinct Identity
    Hrushevsky points out what exactly was erased: the "Synopsis" is "a brief history of Rus composed at the Pechersk Lavra, done very lifelessly and incompletely (the history of the Cossacks, the struggle against Poland, and all of Ukraine's recent history are almost entirely omitted from it)"
  • "Eternal Peace" with Poland (1686) — technique Collusion with a Third Party Behind the People's Back
    Ukrainian historiography recorded the deal as an ordinary purchase - Hrushevsky: Muscovy "concluded an eternal accord with Poland (paying, in addition, another 146 thousand rubles for Kyiv)"
  • Integration Reforms of Peter I and the Great Northern War (1700–1708) — technique Deprivation of Agency
    The Muscovite government had pursued this line from the very beginning"
  • Mazepa's Defection to Sweden and the Baturyn Massacre (1708) — technique Terror
    Hrushevsky: the Muscovite army, having taken Baturyn "through the treachery of one man," "dealt terribly with the populace: the people were slaughtered, the town razed to the ground, the commanders given over to terrible tortures"
  • Mazepa's Defection to Sweden and the Baturyn Massacre (1708) — technique Puppet Government
    Hrushevsky records the mechanics of dependence: "The hetman's residence was moved from Baturyn to Hlukhiv, right up against the Muscovite border, and two Muscovite regiments were garrisoned there so that the residents could arrest the hetman and the officers at once," and "everyone in Ukraine understood that power lay not with the hetman but with those residents"
  • Military Liquidation of the Zaporozhian Sich (1775) — technique Destruction of Historical Memory
    Hrushevsky: "The Sich was destroyed, and on 3 August a tsarist decree announced its abolition, 'with the annihilation of the very name of the Zaporozhian Cossacks'"
  • Crushing of the Cyril and Methodius Society (1847) — technique Neutralization of the Opposition
    …midst of its very formation" after a student's denunciation; "The heaviest punishment fell on Shevchenko: he was given over to the soldiery and exiled to the trans-Caspian desert, forbidden to write and to paint," and "persecutions and prohibitions previously unheard of descended upon Ukrainian thought and the Ukrainian word… the very words Ukraine, Little Russia, Hetmanate were deemed criminal"
  • Formation of the Ideology of Great-Power Chauvinism (1864–1876) — technique Cultivation of a Logic of Superiority
    Hrushevsky records both the thesis and its reach: Valuev invoked an imaginary "majority of Little Russians" who allegedly "know neither a Ukrainian nationality nor language, only one Russian people and tongue," while the ideology's mouthpieces carried it to the formula "there are no Ruthenians — there is only one 'Russian people' from the Carpathians to Kamchatka"
  • Soviet-Ukrainian War: First Invasion and the Puppet Government (December 1917 - April 1918) — technique Mass Killings of Civilians
    Mykhailo Hrushevsky, head of the Central Rada, wrote about the commander in 1921: "the chief commandant, the former gendarme Muravyov, back in February boasted of shooting Ukrainians and of ruining their property"
