Procedures

  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Military Intervention
  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Indiscriminate Attacks
  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Mass Killings of Civilians
    Under sniper fire Chechen families could not collect the bodies of their relatives from the streets and bury them for several days
  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Sham Expression of Popular Will
    On 17 December at nine in the evening, as soon as the polling stations closed, Russian pilots began bombing Gudermes (see: "Indiscriminate Attacks")
  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Sham Corridor
    The crew killed the son of one of the refugee women (see: "Mass Killings of Civilians")
  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Pillage
    Until December 1995, Russian servicemen who served in Gudermes on contract in 45-day shifts got drunk before going home, entered the homes of Chechens and took chickens, geese, carpets and dishes
    Russian soldiers lived in dachas outside the town, came every day into the homes of Chechens and took carpets, dishes, food and Red Cross medicines
  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Aggressor Claiming Victim Status
    On 26 December, Shkirko, the commander of the occupation troops, stated that whoever opens fire first is to blame for the deaths, and that the fighters of the army of Ichkeria attack first
  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Transport Blockade
    On 14 December, the Armed Forces of Ichkeria liberated the city, and on 19-20 December Russian troops captured it (see: "Indiscriminate Attacks")
  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Medical and Humanitarian Blockade
    On the way, refugees froze to death, among them at least four children
    On 21 January 1996, her mother Melkhash Zelimkhanova told human rights defenders of the international organization Human Rights Watch and of "Memorial" there: "When we were in the basement [in Gudermes] it was very cold, and by the time we got here my daughter, Zali, got sick and died a sudden death"
    In December 1995 and January 1996, soldiers at Russian checkpoints hardly ever let through the vehicles of the Belgian section of Médecins Sans Frontières, and the doctors could not get medicines to Gudermes
    The commanders of the occupation troops in Grozny and the military leadership in Moscow did not reply until the end of January to the ICRC's requests to name someone to negotiate with
  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Total Destruction of Infrastructure
    According to refugees and journalists' estimates, the occupiers destroyed or damaged about 40 percent of the buildings of Gudermes
  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Information Isolation
    From 14 to 20 December, after the Armed Forces of Ichkeria had liberated Gudermes, the second-largest city of the republic, the occupiers shelled and bombed the city, and until 25 December did not let the press in (see: "Indiscriminate Attacks")
    In January 1996, the command of the occupation forces closed Grozny and the east of Ichkeria to the press
