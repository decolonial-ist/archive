  • Kolomak Articles (1687) — technique Restriction of Sovereignty
    write anything to anyone"
    The abolition of the historical right to grant asylum: the autonomy was forced to perform the functions of the Muscovite punitive apparatus and to hand over fugitives: "and those fugitives and people of all ranks shall not be received, nor kept among them"
  • Kolomak Articles (1687) — technique Implantation of Officials and Military Personnel
    To ensure constant armed oversight of the autonomy's top leadership and to prevent uprisings, the Tsardom of Muscovy stationed its regular troops directly in the hetman's capital: "with the hetman in Baturyn, for his protection and safety, there shall be a Muscovite streltsy regiment..."
  • Kolomak Articles (1687) — technique Denial of a Distinct Identity
    Under threat of punishment, the population was forbidden even to say aloud that their lands constituted a separate state entity: "and let no one utter such voices, that the Little Russian land is of the hetman's regiment"

  • The Lebedyn Executions (1708–1709) — technique Terror
    Intimidation of the population by an exemplary reprisal against an entire town: during the capture of Baturyn "in the bloody massacre and fire a multitude of the town's defenders and inhabitants perished"
  • Liquidation of the Zaporozhian Sich (1709) — technique Destruction of Historical Memory
    The issuing of an official manifesto banning the very name of the Sich in any documents
  • Liquidation of the Zaporozhian Sich (1709) — technique Justification Through Religion
    The use of the church as an instrument of punishment: the anathema was proclaimed by the tsar's direct order - "be pleased to have him, for such a deed of his, publicly cursed in the cathedral church..."
    No theological grounding was supplied for it, and part of the Ukrainian clergy refused to accept the rite: a former Lokhvytsia archpriest said during the proclamation of the anathema, "Our Mazepa is holy and will be in heaven, and your sovereign will not"
  • The Battle of Poltava and the Final Defeat of the Hetmanate (1709) — technique Military Intervention
    Physical destruction of the armed forces of the supporters of independence in a general battle in order to eliminate the military potential of the resistance
  • The Battle of Poltava and the Final Defeat of the Hetmanate (1709) — technique Terror
    Mass executions of captured Cossacks after the capitulation at Perevolochna and Poltava for the final intimidation of the supporters of autonomy
  • The Battle of Poltava and the Final Defeat of the Hetmanate (1709) — technique Deprivation of Agency
    The rout at Poltava set off an irreversible process of dismantlement: "In the hundred years after the famous Pereiaslav Council, Ukraine traveled the path from the 'Articles of Bohdan Khmelnytsky'… to the de facto liquidation of the autonomy and the hetmanate, to the country's transformation into an ordinary province of the Russian Empire"
  • Forced Labor on Imperial Construction: The Ladoga Canal (1704–1725) — technique Resource Exploitation
    Shifting the upkeep of the mobilized onto the subjects themselves: Cossacks dispatched to the imperial construction sites were left without supply and "suffer great want from hunger, having neither food for themselves, nor winter quarters, nor fodder for the horses"
    Workers for state construction the tsar simply ordered to be "taken from Ukraine, from those towns that are under your authority"
