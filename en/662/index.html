Campaigns

  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996)
    Sadulla Yusupov, an elderly Chechen from Samashki, told Oleg Orlov of the Russian human rights organization Memorial on 13 April: "They were screaming in inhuman voices, swearing, cursing, shouting: 'Come out, you bitches!' and they went up to every house and fired"
    The Observer Mission published a list by name of 103 people whom the occupiers killed in the village in two days (see: "Mass Killings of Civilians")

Procedures

  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Indiscriminate Attacks
    On 2 February artillerymen hit one yard on this street with a mortar and killed a woman and a 14-year-old girl in the morning, and three people at a funeral in the afternoon
    On 26 March the crews of six Russian helicopters fired rockets at Samashki and seriously wounded four people
  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Mass Killings of Civilians
    The occupiers captured Samashki after a short fight with a small group of armed Chechens from the village
    The report of the Observer Mission of Russian human rights organizations says that Yuki Gaitukayeva, Madu Rasuyev and Kesirt Rasuyeva could not get out of the burning house and apparently burned alive
    The mission published a list by name of 103 people whom the occupiers killed in Samashki on 7 and 8 April
  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Collective Punishment
    From 26 February to 3 March 1995 the Russian military at the post near Samashki, a village in the west of Ichkeria, threatened the elders with a strike on the village if they did not persuade their fellow villagers to "settle the matter peacefully"
    The elders fulfilled their obligation, but from 13 March the Russian military at the posts let no one out of Samashki for 25 days and did not let food and medicine through (see: "Transport Blockade")
    Either they burn the house down, or the house perishes, anything at all..."
    On 7 and 8 April the Russian military killed 103 Chechen civilians in Samashki, whom human rights defenders recorded by name (see: "Mass Killings of Civilians")
  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Filtration Camp System
    'You are Chechens — we are going to kill you'… Then they took our sons and drove them away"
  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Torture and Abuse
    Russian soldiers together with police officers beat them at checkpoints and during "zachistki" ("sweeps", as Russians called house searches and detentions of Chechens)
    On 8 April, soldiers of the Internal Troops beat Chechen men and teenagers with rifle butts during searches in Samashki, and at the military unit north of the village and near Assinovskaya soldiers set dogs on them
    During interrogations, the servicemen beat him in the kidneys and the chest, attached wires to his neck and tortured him with electric current, which left burns
    On 10 April, the occupiers sent him by helicopter to the filtration camp in Mozdok, and that day six underage Chechens from Samashki were released from there bruised and with bite marks

  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Killing of Prisoners and Detainees
    On 7-8 April 1995, soldiers of the Internal Troops of the Russian Ministry of Internal Affairs and Russian police officers, during searches in Samashki, a village in the west of Ichkeria, shot detained Chechen men in courtyards, and the Observer Mission of Russian human rights organizations named 27 of them (see: "Mass Killings of Civilians")
    The Russian escort that led detained Chechens from Samashki to a military unit in the hills north of the village shot three of them dead on the way: one of them, wounded, a soldier killed on the order of the escort commander
  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique False Guarantees of Safety
    Russian generals and officers gave their promises to village heads and elders at their posts, by word of mouth or in the form of a written treaty
    General Kosolapov signed the treaty and gave a copy to the head of the village
    On 7 and 8 April, the Russian military killed 103 Chechen civilians in the village, whom human rights defenders recorded by name (see: "Mass Killings of Civilians")
    On 7 April the special forces of Russia's MVD began to enter the village"
  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Sham Corridor
    Of the five thousand people in the village, 100-150 managed to get out before the shelling, and even before the appointed hour the Russian military killed two teenagers and mortally wounded a woman who were leaving the village
  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Expulsion of Foreign Missions
    When deputies and human rights defenders came to inspect the filtration camps, officers of the occupation forces did not let them talk to the detained Chechens
    On 11 April, the Russian military at the checkpoint did not let the marchers into Samashki
    On 7 and 8 April 1995, the Internal Troops of Russia's Ministry of Internal Affairs (predecessors of today's Rosgvardia) stormed Samashki and killed 103 Chechen civilians (see: "Mass Killings of Civilians")
    On 13 April, Kulikov, commander of the occupation forces in Ichkeria, forbade letting into Samashki anyone not registered as a resident of the village, including human rights defenders

  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Lies about Its Own Actions
    On 7 and 8 April, the occupiers killed 103 peaceful Chechens in the village (see: "Mass Killings of Civilians")
    On 29 May, the Russian parliament's commission investigating the causes of the war in Ichkeria found the reports of the shooting of the elders to be a lie
    That day, Soskovets, deputy chairman of the Russian government, answered an NTV correspondent's question about the killings of women and men in the village: "No, there is nothing of the kind"
    Govorukhin, chairman of the Russian parliament's commission investigating the causes of the war, told the deputies after his trip to Samashki on 14 April that he had counted there "about fifty destroyed or damaged houses"

  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Pillage
    From December 1994, Russian police officers and fighters of the Russian Ministry of Internal Affairs looted Chechen families in the stanitsa of Assinovskaya in the west of Ichkeria
    In February, because of the looting in Assinovskaya, the head of the Achkhoy-Martan district in the west of Ichkeria and a representative of the Armed Forces of Ichkeria refused to let the Internal Troops of the Russian Ministry of Internal Affairs into the villages of the district
    The soldiers loaded video equipment, tape recorders, televisions, carpets and furniture into them and into armored personnel carriers, and searched women's necks for gold
    Human rights defenders of the Observer Mission found Sadulla Yusupov's house burned down, but there were no traces of fighting on its walls

  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Aggressor Claiming Victim Status
    On 7 and 8 April, the occupiers killed 103 peaceful Chechens in the village (see: "Mass Killings of Civilians")
    That day, Russian soldiers had fired on the cars in which the elders were travelling, and all eight people survived (see: "Lies about Its Own Actions")
    In July 1995, six of the ten deputies of the Russian parliament's commission that investigated the causes of the war wrote in their conclusion that the command of the occupation troops had decided to storm Samashki "after yet another killing of three servicemen (border guards)"
    The border guards had been killed in January 1995, three months before the storming, in the neighboring stanitsa of Assinovskaya
  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Transport Blockade
    From the end of February 1995, units of the Russian Ministry of Internal Affairs set up checkpoints on the roads between villages in the west of Ichkeria
    13 near the village stopped letting trucks in
    From 13 March 1995, Russian servicemen blocked all the roads into Samashki and for 25 days, until the assault on 7 April, kept the village under blockade
    Some of the men among them Russian servicemen took by helicopter to the filtration camp in Mozdok, while those who had not managed to leave stayed in the village under shelling
  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Medical and Humanitarian Blockade
    13 did not let into the village doctors of the International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC), of Médecins Sans Frontières and of the Russian Red Cross
    On 9 April, Dzeki Musayeva, born in 1920, died of a hypertensive attack without medical help
    The Mission published a list by name of those killed in the village, including those who died of their wounds without medical help
  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Desecration of the Dead
    In captured villages, Russian soldiers threw the bodies of the Chechens they had killed into houses they had set on fire, or doused them with gasoline and set them alight
    On the evening of 7 April, Russian soldiers killed Ali Sugaipov, Emin Nadyrov, Khamzat Kharkharoyev and his son Akhmed, and on the morning of 8 April fellow villagers found their burned bodies in one yard
    Russian tank crews wounded 62-year-old Dogu Tsatishayev, and soldiers shot him dead at point-blank range with an automatic rifle in house 93 on Sharipov Street, poured gasoline over the body and set it on fire
  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Total Destruction of Infrastructure
    In captured villages and towns, Russian soldiers and police officers looted houses (see: "Pillage") and then set them on fire with gasoline, hay or flamethrowers
    Russian servicemen spent the night in the school on Kooperativnaya Street, and in the morning set it on fire, and the school burned out from the inside
    I am left on the street hungry and cold with 4 children"
    The Observer Mission published a list drawn up by the administration of Samashki of 198 yards where the occupiers had completely destroyed at least one house, and in 155 of them almost all the houses had burned down
  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Deprivation of Drinking Water
    On 31 January 1995, the occupiers shelled Samashki, a village in the west of Ichkeria, from tanks and helicopters, leaving the Chechens in the village without water (see: "Indiscriminate Attacks")
    They're all sitting on the other side of the street, sitting, laughing, cracking sunflower seeds"
    That's probably what they were thinking"
  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Information Isolation
    On 7 and 8 April, the Internal Troops of Russia's Ministry of Internal Affairs (predecessors of today's Rosgvardia) stormed Samashki, a village in the west of Ichkeria, and killed 103 Chechen civilians there (see: "Mass Killings of Civilians")
