Campaigns

  • Coercion of Devastated Zandak into Subordination and Tribute by Colonel Pullo (1836)
    In a report to General Petrov dated 27 August 1836 he admitted: "Being certain that the inhabitants of Zandak, following the common custom of the Chechens, would ask for submission after punishment, and considering such submission useful for the government, I did not order the village burned"
    The same report recorded the mechanism of coercion: the devastation of Zandak, which had been considered impregnable, "brought both the inhabitants of the village and all those living in its vicinity into fear and despondency"
    not to permit rebellious gatherings among themselves, to deliver our captives in exchange for their own taken during the devastation of Zandak, and to pay, on a par with the Aukhovites and other submissive tribes, the established tribute"
    Pullo assessed the outcome as the conqueror's gain: "All the above-stated consequences arising from the devastation of the Zandak people have produced important advantages for us"
  • Extermination of the Inhabitants of Kishkeroy-Yurt by Pullo's Detachment: 76 Killed, 40 Captured, Village Burned with Its Stores (December 1836)
    The pretext for the punishment was declared to be hospitality: the village's inhabitants "had harbored the rebel Tashov-Hadji"
    Surrounded at dawn, the village was exterminated together with its people: those fleeing "were caught or exterminated", while "the inhabitants, having barricaded themselves in their houses with their families, were put to the bayonet"
    Rozen himself summed up the outcome: "Upon the occupation of the village of Kishkeroy-Yurt, 76 bodies of the slain were found in it, and 40 souls of both sexes were taken captive; all the property of the inhabitants and the weapons taken from the slain fell to our troops"
    The village of 63 households, "with a considerable stock of grain and hay," was put to the torch in the middle of winter
  • Coercion of Chechnya into Oaths by Feze's Spring Tour: Amanats from Communities Coerced into the Oath, Destruction of Auls along the Dzhalka, a Livestock Fine (February-April 1837)
    General Feze stated the calculation openly - he acted "with the intention of making use, as far as possible, of the panic fear" (report to Velyaminov of 15 March 1837)
  • The amanat-and-tribute system of colonel Pullo in the seized auls of Chechnya (1836-1840)
    Handing over the command, Pullo summed it up in a memorandum of 1840: "by threatening all the unsubmissive communities with the annihilation of their property, I instilled in them such fear, brought them to such a state, that one after another they began to submit"

Procedures

  • Coercion of Devastated Zandak into Subordination and Tribute by Colonel Pullo (1836) — technique Forced Registration of Subjecthood
    The subordination was engineered by the devastation itself: in a report to General Petrov dated 27 August 1836, Pullo admitted: "Being certain that the inhabitants of Zandak, following the common custom of the Chechens, would ask for submission after punishment, and considering such submission useful for the government, I did not order the village burned"
    The same report recorded the state in which the village went to its subordination: the devastation "brought both the inhabitants of the village and all those living in its vicinity into fear and despondency"
    The elders sent "a plea to be accepted into submission to the Russian government", and subjecthood was formalized on terms dictated by the empire: loyalty, a complete break with Tashu-Hadji, the leader of the Chechen resistance, a ban on assemblies, the handover of Russian captives in exchange for the inhabitants of Zandak seized during the devastation, and the established tribute
  • Coercion of Devastated Zandak into Subordination and Tribute by Colonel Pullo (1836) — technique Taxation
    In a report to General Petrov dated 27 August 1836, Pullo recorded the obligation accepted by the elders at the Groznaya fortress: "to pay, on a par with the Aukhovites and other submissive tribes, the established tribute"
    The tribute was not the village's counter-offer but the empire's standing norm for taxing the societies it deemed "submissive"; refusal would have meant the return of the "avenging arms of the Russians" - the village had just survived devastation, the captivity of its inhabitants, and the seizure of its livestock
  • Extermination of the Inhabitants of Kishkeroy-Yurt by Pullo's Detachment: 76 Killed, 40 Captured, Village Burned with Its Stores (December 1836) — technique Punitive Expeditions
    Over the four days of the raid, the village was exterminated together with 76 inhabitants and defenders, and 40 people were led away into captivity
  • Extermination of the Inhabitants of Kishkeroy-Yurt by Pullo's Detachment: 76 Killed, 40 Captured, Village Burned with Its Stores (December 1836) — technique Mass Killings of Civilians
    On 28 December 1836, during the sudden encirclement of Kishkeroy-Yurt, Pullo's troops killed inhabitants who tried to escape, as well as families in their houses: "those seeking salvation in flight were caught or exterminated", "the inhabitants, having barricaded themselves in their houses with their families, were put to the bayonet", and two were smoked out "by straw thrown into the chimneys"
    Upon the occupation of the village, 76 bodies of the slain were found
  • Extermination of the Inhabitants of Kishkeroy-Yurt by Pullo's Detachment: 76 Killed, 40 Captured, Village Burned with Its Stores (December 1836) — technique Total Destruction of Infrastructure
    On 29-31 December 1836, Pullo's detachment completely destroyed Kishkeroy-Yurt: "the village of Kishkeroy, consisting of 63 households, with a considerable stock of grain and hay, was put to the torch", and on withdrawing on 31 December Pullo "marched out of Kishkeroy-Yurt, having set fire to the houses that remained undestroyed"
  • Extermination of the Inhabitants of Kishkeroy-Yurt by Pullo's Detachment: 76 Killed, 40 Captured, Village Burned with Its Stores (December 1836) — technique Artificial Famine
    In the course of the extermination of Kishkeroy-Yurt, Pullo's detachment deliberately destroyed the winter stores: the village was burned "with a considerable stock of grain and hay," and the following day part of the detachment "was occupied with the destruction of the hay stored in the surrounding area"
  • Extermination of the Inhabitants of Kishkeroy-Yurt by Pullo's Detachment: 76 Killed, 40 Captured, Village Burned with Its Stores (December 1836) — technique Abduction of People
    In the course of the extermination of Kishkeroy-Yurt on 28-31 December 1836, Pullo's detachment led away 40 inhabitants into captivity: "40 souls of both sexes were taken captive"
  • Extermination of the Inhabitants of Kishkeroy-Yurt by Pullo's Detachment: 76 Killed, 40 Captured, Village Burned with Its Stores (December 1836) — technique Pillage
    In the course of the extermination of Kishkeroy-Yurt on 28-31 December 1836, Pullo's detachment appropriated the inhabitants' property, including weapons taken from the bodies of the slain: "all the property of the inhabitants and the weapons taken from the slain fell to our troops"

    The system was conceived by Pullo himself: in his 1840 memorandum he wrote that it was then that "a beginning was laid to the system of disarming the highlanders, by levying one gun from every 10 saklias from all the auls"

    In June 1840 the seizure of weapons was fixed as a norm: point 9 of the "Conditions prescribed to submitting highlanders", issued by general Grabbe, commander of the troops of the Caucasus Line, on 15 June 1840, prescribed "To hand over one good rifle from every 10 saklias, as a pledge of their submission" (saklia - a highlander's house)
