Procedures

  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Mass Killings of Civilians
    In March 1996 the UN Secretary-General, in a report to the UN Commission on Human Rights, cited the calculation of Russian human rights organizations: from 25 November 1994 to 15 April 1995, 26,550 civilians died in Ichkeria from Russia's war, among them 2,300 children under 15 and 5,050 women
  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Killing of Prisoners and Detainees
    On 26 March 1996, the UN Secretary-General transmitted to the UN Commission on Human Rights reports that Russian troops had executed captured Chechen men without trial: peaceful people and soldiers of the army of Ichkeria
  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Hostage-Taking
    In his report of 26 March 1996, the UN Secretary-General cited reports by human rights organizations: Russian troops often detained any Chechen man, whether there was evidence against him or not, in order to exchange him for captured Russian soldiers
  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Occupation as Salvation
    The reply says that on 29 August 1995 Yeltsin appointed Lobov, secretary of the Russian Security Council, as his representative in Ichkeria to guarantee human rights and prepare free elections (see: "Sham Expression of Popular Will")
  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Deprivation of Agency
    On 11 January, the Russian government, in a letter to the UN Secretary-General, named the government of Ichkeria as the cause of the war, declaring it illegal and Ichkeria itself part of Russia
  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Aggressor Claiming Victim Status
    In January 1996, the Russian government wrote to the UN Secretary-General that Russia had from the very beginning wanted to settle the matter by negotiations, and that supporters of Dzhokhar Dudayev, the president of Ichkeria, were wrecking the peace process with provocations
  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Transport Blockade
  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Medical and Humanitarian Blockade
    To the south of Ichkeria and to Russian-controlled Dagestan, where Chechens had fled, Russian agencies sent no aid
    To the appeal of the representative of the UN Secretary-General of 7 November 1995 about the shortage of food among the refugees of Sernovodsk, the Russian government replied almost four months later, on 29 February 1996
    Sergei Kovalyov, Russia's human rights commissioner, informed the UN that Russia was obstructing the delivery of humanitarian aid to the war zone, including medicines to save the wounded
  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Administrative Corruption and Extortion
    In the autumn of 1995, Russian soldiers at the checkpoints near Achkhoy-Martan and Sernovodsk, villages in the west of Ichkeria, took from everyone who entered or left the village 100,000 rubles (23 dollars) at Achkhoy-Martan and 50,000 rubles at Sernovodsk
