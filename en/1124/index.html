Author: Комиссии по переговорам Правительства Российской Федерации и Кабинета министров Чеченской Республики Ичкерия

Published in: подписан в Назрани 10.06.1996 руководителями комиссий В. Михайловым и А. Масхадовым при посредничестве представителя ОБСЕ Т. Гульдимана; текст по публикации: Ляховский А. А. Зачарованные свободой: Тайны кавказских войн. Москва: Детектив-Пресс, 2006. С. 398-400 (документ 67)

Procedures

  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Sham Expression of Popular Will
    On 10 June 1996, in Nazran, a town in Russian-controlled Ingushetia, the delegations of Russia and Ichkeria recorded in the ceasefire protocol that elections would be held after the withdrawal of Russian troops and the disarmament of the army of Ichkeria
  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Sham Ceasefire
    On 27 May, Russian prime minister Chernomyrdin and Zelimkhan Yandarbiyev, acting president of Ichkeria, signed a ceasefire agreement in Moscow, and on the evening of 3 June Russian pilots bombed villages of the Nozhay-Yurt and Vedeno districts in the south-east of Ichkeria
    On 10 June in Nazran, Russia undertook to stop the bombing and to remove its checkpoints by 7 July, and on 9 July, six days after Yeltsin's victory in the Russian presidential election, the occupiers launched a new offensive
  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Negotiations as Cover for an Attack
    On 3 July 1996, in Novye Atagi, a village south of Grozny, the Russian members of the Russia-Ichkeria working group on military questions, headed by General Tikhomirov, declared that they were removing the checkpoints and withdrawing military units from Ichkeria
  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Aggressor Claiming Victim Status
    On 9 July 1996, a month after Russia and Ichkeria agreed on a ceasefire in Nazran, a town in Russian-controlled Ingushetia, the occupiers began a new offensive (see: "Sham Ceasefire")
  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Sham Treaties
    On 10 June, in Nazran, a town in Russian-controlled Ingushetia, the delegations of Russia and Ichkeria supplemented it with a protocol in which Russia undertook to withdraw its troops from the republic by the end of August 1996
  • First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996) — technique Transport Blockade
    The Russian delegation undertook to remove the checkpoints at the exits from villages and towns by 7 July
