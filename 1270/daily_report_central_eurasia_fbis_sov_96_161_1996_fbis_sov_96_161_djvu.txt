{[PBIS Transcribed Text] MOSCOW, Aug 16 (Interfax)

An International Non-Governmental Tribunal on
Crimes Against Humanity and War Crimes in Chechnya
will begin work in November or December 1996, Sergey
Grigor; ants, chairman of the Glasnost public fund for
protection of journalists and chairman of the organizing
committee to create the Tribunal, told a news conference
in Moscow on Friday

The Tribunal will consider the responsibility of high-
ranking Russian officials for such crimes, he said

The organizing committee to create the Tribunal in
particular includes State Duma deputy Valeriy Borschev
and writer Anatoi:y Pristavkin of the Russian PEN-
Center

The Tribunal will consider such events as a Russian-
sponsored anu-Dudayev offensive on the Chechep cap-
ital of Groznyy in November 1994, the assault on
Groznyy in December 1994 and January 1995 by Rus-
sian federal forces, bomb raids and artillery attacks on
Groznyy and the settlements of Shali and Bamut, Grig-
oryants told Interfax

The Tribunal will also discuss the “clean-up” operation
in the village of Samashkiy on April 7-8, 1995, and
developments in the town of Budennovsk, southern
Russia. where Chechen gunmen launched a hostage-
taking raid in June 1995, Grigoryants said

The situation concerning children, problems relating to
filtration camps, irregularities regarding the freedom to
receive and circulate information, the destruction of
cultural treasures and the use of banned kinds of mass
destruction weapons will also be considered, he said

The Tribunal is likely to hold the proceedings in
London, Grigoryants said. Other possible venues for
the Tribunal’s sessions include Nuremburg. Berlin,
Bordeaux and Warsaw. A decision on the venue for the
Tribunal’s sessions will be made in October, he said

The Tribunal is made up of 14 people, including a
former chairman of the European Parliament's Human
Rights Committee, a Nobel prize winner, a former U.S
deputy secretary of state, and others

from their captors.

Russia's embassy to the UAE has been in contact with
them constantly.

ists on Saturday that negotiations on their soonest return
to Russia were in progress. They will return today or
tomorrow, he said.

credit first of all, Shaymiyev said. “Russian agencies
