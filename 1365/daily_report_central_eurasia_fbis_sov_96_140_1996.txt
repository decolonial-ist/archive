[Press Review" — ITAR-TASS headline)

(FBIS Transcribeu Text] MOSCOW, July 19 (Itar-Tass)
— Follows a review of Friday's Moscow-based Russian
newspapers dated July 19. The press highlights a search
for MIA in Chechnya, the hostage release issue, the
economic and political situation in Russia, and housing
construction.

Newspapers report that the Olympic flame is to be
lighted in Atlanta on Friday at the opening of the
X¥“Y1l Olympic Games, at which Russian Olympians
will perform as an independent team for the first time
since 1912.

IZVESTIYA carries an interview with Colonel-General
Valentin Panichev, Chief Military Prosecutor, and his
staff about problems connected with attempts to find
evidence of corruption in the top echelon of the Defence
Ministry.

KRASNAYA ZVEZDA runs remarks by President
Boris Yeltsin at a visiting session of the Defence
Ministry Collegium at the Barvikha sanatorium near
Moscow. The President introduced Colonel-General
Igor Rodionov, the newly-appointed Defence Minister
of the Russian Federation, to the Armed Forces’ top
brass.

KRASNAYA ZVEZDA on a search for MIA in Chech-
nya and endeavours to secure the release of captives
held in Chechnya.

“We do everything to secure the release of our soldiers
from captivity ip Chechnya,” points out State Duma
Member Nikoiai Bezborodov, Head of the Group for the
search for those missing in action and for the freeing of
captives held in Chechnya. As of now, 467 people have
been freed, with 366 servicemen among them.

IZVESTIYA on the re-emergence of Chechen field
commander Salman Raduyev. Under the headline
“Salman Raduyev is alive, is back in Chechnya and is
preparing yet another act of sabotage”, the newspaper
carries a dispatch by its correspondent Vadim Belykh
from Chechnya.

The journalist reports that field commander Raduyev,
who gained notoriety after the militants’ armed actions
at Kizlyar and Pervomayskoye, was transported in
an utterly serious condition across the territory of
Azerbaijan to one of Middle Eastern countries. Over
there, he was healed of his wounds, and had a nose
restoration operation performed on him. Early in July,
Raduyev returned to Chechnya and again assumed the
duties of the commander of the south-western front, the
journalist writes.

ROSSIYSKIYE VESTI opens up a new rubric, “Ideas
for Russia".

“There are ideas that can unite everyone” is the headline
of an article by Gennadiy Osipov, Director of the
Russian Academy of Sciences’ Institute for Socio-
Political Studies.

ROSSIYSKAYA GAZETA runs a Russian government
resolution "On measures to protect the consumer market
of the Russian Federation from penetration by sub-
quality imported merchandise”.

ROSSIYSKAYA GAZETA carries a column of dis-
patches by its own correspondents about the course of
the implementaion of the Federal Housing Programme.
They point out that housing construction is in progress
not only in Moscow but also in Tyumen, Vladimir,
Orenburg, and other cities. In all, 124 million square
metres of housing have been built in the country over
the past three years, and seven million people in Russia
have been rehoused.

TRUD runs an interview with Kyrgyz President Askar
Akayev, who believes that “Russia, Kazakhstan, and
Uzbekistan are the three countries on which Kyrgyzs-
tan’s future depends”. Akayev emphasised that Russia
has been and will remain Kyrgyzstan's main ally and
partner.

SEGODNYA runs an interview with Nikolay Yegorov,
former Chief of the Presidential Staff, who has been
appointed the Governor of Krasnodar Territory, makes
an estimate of the economic and political situation in
Russia.

(FBIS Transcribed Text) Riga, Jul 17, BNS — Latvian
Foreign Minister Valdis Birkavs is reserved toward the
visit of 11 Taiwanese parliamentarians to Latvia.

He told a press conference on Tuesday that the visit does
not change Latvia's foreign policy line “two Chinas —
one state.”

Birkavs said that Latvia's foreign policy concept pro-
vides for government-level contacts with China, but not
with Taiwan. In addition, Latvia does not recognize sov-
ereignty of Taiwan and both countries do not have diplo-
matic relations.

A Taiwanese consulate was operating in Latvia from
February of 1992 to July of 1994, and then its status
was changed into that of a mission. Shortly before that,
Latvia's relations with China had improved.

Birkavs noted that the European Union (EU) countries
do not recognize independence of Taiwan, and “Latvia
has to respect those values which it strives to take over.”
Latvia could change its political attitude toward Taiwan
if it was decided by the EU, said the minister.

A delegation of the Taiwanese parliament arrived in
Latvia on Tuesday and will stay in Latvia until July 19.
The guests will meet with representatives of the Sacima,
parliament factions and the University of Latvia.

Lithuania: IAEA Head: Vilnius, Moscow Must
Cooperate in Nuclear Power

LD1807111496 Moscow INTERFAX in English
1019 GMT 18 Jul 96

(FBIS Transcribed Text) VILNIUS, July 18 (Interfax)
— Lithuania must continue to cooperate with Russia in
the field of nuclear power engineering, Director-General
of the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA)
Hans Blix said at a meeting with President Algirdas
Brazauskas in Vilnius.

Such cooperation is necessary, Blix said, since Lithua-
nia’s biggest nuclear power station in Ignalina uses
Russian-made reactors, and it is more convenient for
Lithuania to buy nuclear fuel in Russia, the president's
chancellery told Interfax on Thursday.

Lithuania has neither economic nor financial opportu-
nities to stop the operation of the Ignalina station, Blix
said.

The Ignalina station is safer than the one in Chernobyl,
he said. Lithuania has done much in the last few years
to strengthen the safety of its nuclear power station with
the help of foreign specialists and credits, he said.

The two men also discussed ways to store nuclear waste
and prospects for investment in electricity production in
Lithuania.

Blix will visit the Ignalina station on Thursday to inspect
its work and review safety problems.
