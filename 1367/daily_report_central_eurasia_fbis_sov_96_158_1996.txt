Russia: Deputy Foreign Minister Denies
Yeltsin-Clinton NATO Deal

LD1408110896 Moscow INTERFAX in English
1056 GMT 14 Aug 96

{[FBIS Transcribed Text] MOSCOW, Aug 14 (Interfax)
— Moscow made no attempt to barter a deal with W ash-
ington in which NATO would admit some Eastern Eu-
ropean countries while barring others, Russia's Deputy
Foreign Minister Nikolai Afanasyevskiy [as received]
told Interfax Wednesday. “The position of the Russian
party remains unchanged,” he said.

He said he does not know anything about a secret
letter of President Boris Yeltsin which, accord g to
some foreign press reports, U.S. President Bill Clinton
allegedly received several days ago.

Yeltsin would allegedly agree to the admittance of
Poland, the Czech Republic, and Hungary to NATO
provided the Baltic states are excluded from the process
of NATO's enlargement, according to the reports.

"I have no notion about the existence of such a letter
I think the matter concerns a distorted interpretation of
Russia's positon,” Afanasyevskiy said.

As before, Moscow believes that it 1s necessary to agree
on a general security structure in Europe and to see what
NATO's transformation means, in order to discuss the
nature of mutual relations between Russia and the North
Atlantic Alliance.

“We think that we must negotiate with NATO. We are
open for such agreements and such talks. However, the
issue stating that Russia agrees to the admittance of
several countries to NATO and opposes entering the
alliance by several other countries is absolutely invalid,’
Afanasyevskiy said.

“We stick to the opinion that one should look for a
system of security which will be good for all European
states,” he said.

Georgia: CIS Mandate Extended in Abkhaz Conflict

LD1308122996 Moscow INTERFAX in English
1206 GMT 13 Aug 96

([FBIS Transcribed Text] MOSCOW, Aug 13 (Interfax)
— Georgia and its breakaway province of Abkhazia
have agreed to extend the mandate of the CIS joint
peacekeeping forces in the zone of the Georgian-
Abkhazian conflict until January 31, 1997, Russian
Foreign Ministry spokesman Mikhail Demurin told a
briefing in Moscow on Tuesday [13 August).

Georgia and Abkhazia agreed to extend the peacekeep-
ers’ mandate, which ran out on July 19, during their
talks in Moscow in late July and early August, the
spokesman said.

The Georgian-Abkhazian talks were brokered by Russia,
which also supplies all of the soldiers for the CIS force.

Georgia and Abkhazia managed to work out “a word-
ing which takes into consideration both sides’ positions”
concerning the amendment of the peacekeepers’ man-
date, the spokesman said.

Besides other powers, the peacekeepers will have the
powers to take measures to prevent or stop the activities
of “terrorist or sabotage formations and other armed
groups” in order to ensure the security of refugees in
the Gali district of Abkhazia (within its former borders),
the spokesman said.

If requested to do so, the peacekeepers will also ensure
the security of employees of the U.N. mission in Geor-
gia, the OSCE and other international organizations, the
spokesman said.

These agreements will be submitted for consideration
by the CIS heads of state in order to make a decision
under the existing procedure, the spokesman said.

(FBIS Translated Text} Moscow, 13 Aug (IPRINDA)
— Reports were circulated by the mass media that
mandate of the peacekeepers deployed in the Georgian-
Abkhaz conflict zone has been extended by decision of
the Russian Federation Council.

R. Sichinava, envoy extraordinary and plenipotentiary to
Russia for the above matters, told an IPRINDA corre-
spondent that extension of the mandate is a prerogative
of the Council of Heads of State and that the Federation
Council only gave the right for further use of the joint
peacekeeping forces in the conflict zone The destiny of
the mandate is to be decided in about two weeks’ time.

According to the Georgian diplomat, a new version of
the mandate, which was agreed with both the Georgian
and the Abkhaz sides, will be sent to the heads of the
CIS states and their decision will decide the fate of the
mandate.

R. Sichinava also noted that the Georgian side will
agree to extension of the mandate providing Georgia's
proposals for an unconditional return of the refugees to
Gali district are taken into consideration.

According to the same source, Russian Deputy Foreign
Minister Boris Pastukhov, Russian Deputy Prime
Minister A. Bolshakov, Georgian Ambassador V.
Lortkipanidze, and members of the Georgian Govern-
ment will visit Gali district in the near future to discuss
with the Abkhaz side the possibilities for the refugees
to return to the region.
