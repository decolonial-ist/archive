(FBIS Transcribed Text] MOSCOW July 18 (Itar-Tass)
— Follows a review of Thursday's Moscow-based
Russias newspapers dated July 18.

ROSSIYSKIYE VESTI on CPRF intentions to press for
an early presidential election.

The newspaper carries an interview with Sergey
Sh<chray, member of the State Duma, who stated
that “the Communist Party of the Russian Federation
(CPRF) intends, through the efforts of the State Duma
and with the backing of the party's regional structures,
to frustrate the realisaiton of the President's pre-poll
promises, to precipitate a socio-economic crisis in the
autumn of 1996 so as to organise a campaign in spring
1997 for a pre-term presidential election”.

IZVESTIYA on the situation of Chechnya.

The newspaper's special correspondent Valeriy Yakov,
in a dispatch from Chechen villages “cleared from
militants", expresses the view that thereby Federal
forces carried out “special operations against peace in
Chechnya”.

The correpsondent seeks to prove that 13 killed Chechen
civilians in Groznyy’s suburb were shot to death by Fed-
eral soldiers. Following the “clearing” operations and
the killings, “one can hardly expect that Security Coun-
cil Secretary Aleksandr Lebed will fly into Chechnya
with peace and that punitive operations will be termi-
nated,” Yakov “ites.

IZVESTIYA on conflict between Russian ministries of
the Defence Industry and Defence over U.S request.

The newspaper's analyst Viktor Litovkin reports that
a conflict is flaring up between the Russian Ministry
of the Defence Industry and the Ministry of Defence
over a U.S. insistent request for the sale of a super-
modern anti-ship missile Mosquitto to it. The missile
is notable for a unique destructive potential and is
particularly effective against aircraft carriers. Navymen
firmly oppose the idea of selling the missile, whereas
the missile makers are in favour of granting the request
because this would enable them to survive financially.

ROSS!IYSKAYA GAZETA on energy crisis in Komi
and Maritime Territory.

the Republic of Komi and Maritime Territory. The Far
East power producers have not received 1,500,000 mil-

of firm partnership. I feel that Russians share these
sentiments. The job of our commission is to create the
practical conditions for firm partnership.”

Russian government chairman Viktor Chernoniyrdin
supported this high appraisal of the commission's work
and stressed: “We are confident now as never before
that we are on the right road." He assessed the Russian
presidential result as “another advance”, given by the
people to the government and gave his assurance that
he will do everything to guarantee a worthy life to all
Russians.

The speeches testified to the great personal feeling be-
tween the Russian premier and the U.S. Vice President.
V. Chernomyrdin referred to the mutual understanding
that has developed as one of the most significant results
of the commission's work. "I am proud of my friend-
ship with Viktor Stepanovich. I can sincerely admit that

Gore Panel

[Report by Aleksey Portanskiy: "Gore-Chernomyrdin

(FBIS Translated Text] The seventh session of the
Russo-U.S. Commission for Economic and Technologi-
cal Cooperation chaired by Russian Prime Minister Vik-
tor Chernomyrdin and U.S. Vice President Al Gore is
being held in Moscow 15-16 July.

The continuation of economic reform in Russia (fol-
lowing Boris Yeltsin's presidential election victory), the
prospects for NATO expansion, and the situation in
Chechnya are the main points on the agenda for the
current meeting, along with routine issues of bilateral
cooperation.

The Gore-Chernomyrdin Commission was set up in
1993 in accordance with a decision reached at the
Russo-American summit in Vancouver, and since then
has met twice a year, alternating its sessions between
the two countries. Initially its purview covered power
generation, space, and advanced technology, but now it
is considerably broader.

Among the more than 100 documents signed by the two
sides we can pick cut the agreement on Russian access
to international commercial rocket launch markets, the
memorandum on cooperation in the conversion sphere,
the agreement on the closure of reactors producing
plutonium, and othe. s.

The weightiest result of the previous commission ses-
sions was the signing of an agreement on the “Sakhalin-
1" project, which envisages $15 billion of investment
being made in Russia. An important achievement of the
commission was the agreement in 1995 of the issue of
Russia's participation as a founder country in the new
post-COCOM [Coordinating Committee for Multilateral
Export Controls} international organization for export
controls (the Wassenaar Arrangement), as well as Rus-
sia’s admission to the international Missile Technology
Control Regime (MTCR).

But the commission has far more unsolved problems
than it has actual successes. Further work will have

to be done to implement the 1994 agreement on the
sale to the United States of Russian weapons-grade

{Article by Yevgeniy Vasilchuk: “Starved Economy
Ready To Fling Itself on Printing Presses")

({FBIS Translated Excerpt] It is already two weeks since
the presidential election. The preelection expectations
and postelection euphoria have given way to a steadfast
feeling of disappointment. In the sphere of the economy
and market reforms the country continues to mark time.

Contrary to the rumors that are multiplying with every
passing day, the government is in no hurry to publicize
the priorities of its future economic policy. The main
principles of the formation and makeup of the new
cabinet remain unclear. Admittedly, not very much time

has passed. But, taking account of the leadership's stated
continuity, this protracted interlude is beginning to drag.

President Yeltsin has pointed to the need to shift
the emphasis in economic regulation toward ensuring
economic growth. Aleksandr Livshits, the president's
economic adviser, has made it clear that this will
require financial flows to be channeled into productive
investment.

But financial flows are rapidly running dry and are
stubbornly reluctant to move into production. The
burden of wasteful state spending and debts is keeping
interest rates at levels that the real sector of the economy
is unable to bear. Despite the authorities’ efforts, rates
are simply refusing to move down. The election results
did not impress the financial markets.

Having proprietorially removed competitors from the
domestic capital market, the state now intends to lure
Western private investors into its web. It should come
as no surprise that the titanic efforts made by Russian
industrialists, bankers, and civil servants to cobble
together half-way efficient financial-industrial groups
have, with some rare exceptions, come to nothing.

The creeping banking crisis has been a stab in the back
for production. A carve-up of the banks’ client base
has begun and could have awful consequences. While
striving to avoid the worst, the authorities and banking
circles are avoiding recognizing the obvious. But the
failure of another couple of major banks would spark
panic among clients. And then things might be too late.
(passage omitted]

The Central Bank clearly lacks any realization that the
Russian banks’ main problem lies not in the “quality”
of certain credit institutions but in the “fragility” of the
entire system. Just as, incidentally, it lacks any clear
idea of ways of combating this looming catastrophe.

The Bank of Russia's problems pale before the concerns
of the government. Tax receipts are not just low but
falling steadily month on month. The treasury can barely
rake up half the revenue due to it. Electoral spending
promises will have to be met. At least 20-30 trillion
rubles more will be needed. Within the next two or
three months at least.

IMF experts are inclined to put the budget deficit in the
first half of 1996 at 10-11 percent of GDP. The strain
on the domestic financial market has been such that the
only way out of this situstion could be to “quietly” print
money. If the financial markets never get wind of it, the
inflationary consequences of this step will be minimal.

The prediction is that the country will continue to
run a very burdensome [as published] trade surplus.
