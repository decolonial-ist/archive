UN Cuts Ukraine, Belarus Annual Contribution

LD2712195394 Moscow INTERFAX in English 1730
GMT 27 Dec 94

[From the “Diplomatic Panorama” feature: Report by
Aleksandr Korzun, Igor Porshnev, and Yevgeniy
Terekhov]}

{FBIS Transcribed Text] Kiev, Dec 27 (Interfax-
Ukraine}—The United Nations has decided to reduce
the size of the annual contribution to its budget from
Ukraine and Be arus, deputy head of the international
agencies department Vladimir Yelchenko told a news
briefing in Kiev on Tuesday [27 December].

The two countries’ contribution will be cut from 1.87
percent to 1.09 percent of the UN budget in the next
three years. Yelchenko said this will help Ukraine to save
$86 million in the next few years.

Cooperation

29 Dec 94 pl

(Unattributed report: “Relations With Russia’

[FBIS Translated Text] In the course of the 27 December
meeting between Ukrainian President Leonid Kuchma
and chairman of the Belarusian Supreme Council
Myacheslaw Gryb much attention was given to relations
with Russia.

“| have always been in favor of a most close integration
between Ukraine, Belarus, and Russia. Even now, I do
not retract anything I have said to that effect. It is simply
impossible for our people to emerge from the crisis
without a more extensive integration,” Leonid Kuchma
told reporters after the meeting. “However, love must be
reciprocal. We must finally sit down at a negotiating
table and see who actually wants integration and does
not.” In the Ukrainian president’s view, Russia is not
always ready for constructive cooperation.

Denmark’s Helveg Petersen on EU Chechnya
Protest

BR0401153295 Copenhagen BERLINGSKE TIDENDE
in Danish 4 Jan 95 p 12

[Report on interview with Danish Foreign Minister
Niels Helveg Petersen by Jesper Strudsholm; place and
date not given: “EU Warning to Russia Came Late”’]

{[FBIS Translated Text] Through the German ambas-
sador in Moscow the EU [European Union] countries
have protested to Russia over the bloodbath in Chech-
nya. But internal disagreement among the EU countries
seems to have prevented any protest being made until
many civilians had been killed.

INTERNATIONAL AFFAIRS 1

Not until 30 December was Ambassador Otto von der
Gablentz sent to the Russian Foreign Ministry on behalf
of the EU with a protest about the killing of civilians.

In an interview with BERLINGSKE TIDENDE Danish
Foreign Minister Niels Helveg Petersen (Radical Liberal
Party) said that as early as 26 or 27 December—he is not
wholly sure about the date—Denmark had been pressing
for a joint EU protest.

[Strudsholm] Why did you wait until the tanks were
already on the streets of Chechnya?

{[Helveg Petersen] There has been a clear escalation of
what is happening militarily. Now criticism is appro-
priate. The latest horrific pictures on television are
documentary evidence that the military action being
taken is disproportionate.

[Strudsholm] But surely people have been able to see
what was coming for a long time?

[Helveg Petersen] I have the whole time considered
intervention both unwise and dangerous, because the
situation could easily develop into what we have seen—
when the civilian population are those worst affected.

[Strudsholm] Why then did you wait until this develop-
ment actually came?

[Helveg Petersen] You can always discuss timing. But it
was only at that point in time that agreement within the
EU could be achieved.

[Strudsholm] Who was dragging their feet?

[Helveg Petersen] I have no comment on that. [Helveg
Petersen ends]

Niels Helveg Petersen said that the protest to the Rus-
sians is based on the current situation. But this does not
necessarily mean that the EU will now intervene more
robustly in the way Russia treats its minorities.

{Strudsholm] Is this a first step toward supporting
Chechen independence—in the same way that we sup-
ported the Baltic countries?

[Helveg Petersen] I strongly oppose that comparison. We
never recognized that the Baltic countries were part of
the Soviet Union. Chechnya has never been recognized
as an independent state.

[Strudsholm] But in both cases the Russians incorpo-
rated them by force.

[Helveg Petersen] In our relations with Russia we recog-
nize that Chechnya is part of the Russian Federation.
But this does not mean that you can disregard the rules
about how you behave toward your population.

[Strudsholm] How do you view Yeltsin's chances to
survive what is happening,?

[Helveg Petersen] I do not want to comment.

U.S. Media Calls for Return to ‘Reaganism’ Noted
MM0501092395 Moscow TRUD in Russian 5 Jan 95 p 5

[Article by Vissarion Sisnev: “Back—to ‘Reaganism?”’
Events in Chechnya Threaten Cooling of U.S.-Russian
Relations”’]

[FBIS Translated Text] Washington—There is no point
in even listing all the U.S. mass media that are nowadays
underlining the striking discrepancy between Moscow's
official statements regarding the situation and the steps
being taken in Chechnya on the one hand, and journal-
ists’ reports from Groznyy on the other hand. U.S.
politicians and public are receiving first-hand informa-
tion from Chechnya so to speak: Newspapers and televi-
sion companies have direct reports from there, including
videos and photographs that give a vivid idea of the
ferocity of the ongoing battles, the devastation, and the
disaster that has befallen innocent people.

In this connection the U.S. Department of State has
expressed ““deep concern” and stressed that the Russian
Government is responsible for the fact that “people not
involved in the hostilities are in danger.”

At the same time people in the United States are also
noticing that, for the first time in many decades, organs
of power and the press in Russia are taking up diamet-
rically opposed positions on such a topical issue as
Chechnya. Whereas the former are striving to gloss over
the gravity of the situation, the media are providing
readers, listeners, and viewers with an objective picture
in both their reports from the Caucasus and their edito-
rial commentaries. THE NEW YORK TIMES Pub-
lishing House Press Service has disseminated an article
headlined: “Russian Press Demonstrates Indepen-
dence.” The article begins with the following words:
“Russian newspapers are justifying their avowed liberal
orientation.” And the article ends: “The Russian mass
media have nothing to be ashamed of in their coverage of
the war. They are manifesting a desire to show both sides
of the events, for honest analysis, and to uphold their
viewpoint.”

Needless to say, comparative analysis of the positions
espoused by official Moscow and the Russian press on
the Chechen issue is not being done simply in order to
give our fellow journalists an approbatory slap on the
back. Being pragmatists, Americans are wondering how
and to what extent this phenomenon concerns their
country and what should be done by way of response, if
one is necessary. And I am hearing increasingly fre-
quently that the need for such a response is coming to a
head. Yeltsin seems to have been right in predicting that
U.S.-Russian relations might become complex after the
Republicans’ victory in the congressional elections.
Judging from the recent statement made by Senate
Majority Leader Robert Dole, U.S. aid for Russia will be
linked with our country’s observance of human rights.
He hinted that the “military operations and death of

1539 GMT 4 Jan 95

(FBIS Transcribed Text] 04/01/95 (NOYAN TAPAN)—
The Armenian Democratic Party issued a statement,
condemning the recent President’s decree on temporary
suspension of the ARF-“Dashnaktsutyn” Party. The
Democratic Party of Armenia views this move as being
not only an encroachment upon the political organiza-
tion but also as violation of principles of democracy,
moreover, this fact reminds the “black days” of the
recent past.

The Armenian Democratic Party also considers that the
presidential decree contradicts Armenia’s legislation,
since only Supreme Court is entitled to suspend or
liquidate this or that political organization. “As a matter
of fact, the main safeguard of lawfulness in the country
violates the law. The move, taken by President proves
that concentration of power in one hands is fraught with
serious aftermath”, the statement reads.

“The Decree pursues the aim to turn people’s attention
from social and economic hardships and current moral
and psychological situation as well as from President's
unfulfilled promises. By this move the atmosphere of
terror and fear, and autocracy, which will enable Presi-
dent and his supporters to rule until the year of 2000 is
being created.

“The Democratic Party of Armenia can not ignore the
fact that President’s move exacerbates the split in the
nation and makes hard impact on Armenian Cause and
defensive potentials of Karabakh. The history does not
have any example, when lawful state was established
with violation of all legal norms.

“The Democratic Party of Armenia condemns any form
of terrorism, be it individual or state terrorism. Any
decree, contradicting the legislation, must not be in
force, therefore President must immediately announce
the decree invalid”, the statement reads.

Aide: Ban Will Defuse Tension

LD0401184195 Moscow INTERFAX in English 1546
GMT 4 Jan 95

[From the “Presidential Bulletin” feature: compiled by
Nikolay Zherebtsov, Andrey Petrovskiy, and edited by
Viadimir Shishlin]}

{[FBIS Transcribed Text] President Levon Ter-
Petrosyan’s decree (of December 28) to suspend the
activity by the opposition Dashnaktsutyun Party will not
upset the country’s internal stability, Presidential Aide
Levon Zurabyan told Interfax.

In fact, he said, the decree is aimed at defusing tension
and preventing terrorist attacks in the republic. IF note:
Dashnaktsutyun is accused of “setting up its own mili-
tant groups, carrying out assassinations of political
leaders and planning other terrorist attacks,” running an
international drug trafficking route from Beirut to
Moscow via Yerevan and establishing illegal economic
structures and engagement in extortion racket. It is also
accused of setting up its own military intelligence service
complete with a network of agents engaged in gathering
sensitive information of military and economic nature.

Ter-Petrosyan in a recent speech on national television
accused the party's intelligence service of involvement in
the assassination of former KGB Chief Marius
Yuzbashyan two years ago and former Mayor of Yerevan
Ambartsum Galstyan in December.

In the meantime, the Dashnaktsutyun party bureau has
denied the charges describing them as a fake up.

In a statement made available to Interfax last weekend,
the party leadership accused the president of exceeding
his powers while suspending activities by Dashnaktsu-
tyun.

Party leaders called on their rank and file members in
and outside Armenia for restraint warning against any
steps that might worsen the country’s economic and
social plight. They said that no presidential decrees
could deprive the party of its right to work for the welfare
of the people.

Head of the parliamentary commission for legal matters
Kim Balayan says the decree is not legally binding. In an

Mayor’s Murder Causes Parliamentary Crisis

Russian 23 Dec 94 p I

[News Department report: “Will the Supreme Council
Take Us All With It? The Crisis Continues To Inten-
sify’’]

[FBIS Translated Text] The assassination of Ambartsum
Galstyan, the former mayor of Yerevan, has become the
cause for a crisis of the Supreme Council, which is of
unprecedented intensity and which will, evidently, be
hard to overcome. Deputies of a number of factions have

announced their resignation from parliament or a sus-
pension of their activity. They include members from
the Union of Action and the National Democratic Union
and members of parliament of the National Crisis and
Free Members of Parliament groups. They will be joined
by certain communist deputies also, possibly. Thus, the
total number of “refuseniks” could reach 50-55.

Of course, the reasons for the crisis are very profound,
and A. Galstyan’s tragic death merely served as the
“primer” for the present events. The chronic absence of
a quorum, the inability (or unwillingness) to work pains-
takingly on the creation of the legal field, the low level of
competence of many of the people's elect—all this could
not have failed sooner or later to have been reflected in
the overall situation. But the fact that parliament has
begun to “die” not only before its appointed time but
without having even discussed here either the new elec-
tion act or the draft future constitution, work on the
reconciled text of which will become bogged down once
again, it has to be thought, would appear highly negative.
It is not hard to imagine how this could be reflected in
the overall situation in the country and in society and in
the authority of the representative power as a whole.

The main demand of those who are refusing to take part
in the work of the Supreme Council is the resignation of
the Presidium and the speaker of parliament. They are to
blame, allegedly, for the inadequate organization of
business and the low capability of the parliamentary
structures. It is not for us to judge how far such charges
correspond to reality. But it has to be noted that many of
the “refuseniks” are themselves the leaders and mem-
bers of a whole number of standing commissions and are
members of the Presidium. In addition, whereas it is
customary to maintain that the bloc of parties of the
right quite dependably controls the parliamentary
majority, this cannot be said about the Presidium. It is
for this reason, evidently, that the one-sided charges of
“disorganization of work” cannot in themselves be con-
sidered the truth in the last instance. It takes two to
tango, as they say, and it would be very odd were the
speaker of parliament and the members of the Presidium
who are members of the “rightwing bloc”’ to purposively
develop the work of the legislative body, overcoming the
resistance of their opponents here, merely so as to
become the target of their criticism.

It would seem that A. Galstyan’s assassination has been
used by the parliamentary opposition for further pres-
sure on the executive branch of power headed by the
president. Halting the work of the Supreme Council at
this time would mean first and foremost the killing off of
discussion of the draft new budget and an attempt
thereby to tie the government's hands in the assimilation
of foreign credit and to thereby discredit it. And, in the
event of it getting its wishes and having objectionable
leaders removed, taking part also in a new “division of
the pie”: for money is already coming from overseas...

All this is being accompanied by an inflammation of an
atmosphere of fear and uncertainty and talk about the

Parliament Deputies Resign, Form Assembly

954F0742B Yerevan RESPUBLIKA ARMENIYA in
Russian 24 Dec 94 p 1

[News Department report: “Is a ‘Shadow’ Structure
Being Formed?”]

[FBIS Translated Text] As already reported, a news
conference of deputies of the Supreme Council who have
united in the Deputies’ Assembly was held yesterday.
Paruyr Ayrikyan, Shavarsh Kocharyan, Seyran Bag-
dasaryan, and Aibert Bagdasaryan took part.

In their opinion, a totalitarian regime is gradually being
established in the republic, political terrorism and kill-
ings are becoming commonplace occurrences, the indi-
vidual’s right to life is being ignored, the vast majority of
the people are condemned to a beggarly existence, and
the state has become an instrument for the retention of
power by the present leadership of the republic. Not only
the upcoming parliamentary elections but also the future
of the entire people are in jeopardy.

Under these conditions, deputies elected by the people
and unwilling to submit to the powers that be consider it
their duty to adopt measures to restore the prestige of the
legislative body and its ability to work.

Deputies from the Union of Action and the National
Democratic Union and the Free Members of Parliament
and National Progress groups totaling thus far 47 per-
sons, while not participating in the business of the
Supreme Council, have at the same time no intention of
terminating their parliamentary activity, their political
activity even less. In addition, the deputies demand the
resignation of the Presidium of the Supreme Council and
its speaker Babken Ararktsyan. It was announced at the
news conference also that the “refuseniks” could be
joined by individual deputies from the Communist Party
of Armenia.

believe it essential to convene on Monday, 26 December,

Karabakh Conflict Refugee Statistics Reported

954F0742C Yerevan RESPUBLIKA ARMENIYA in
Russian 24 Dec 94 p 1

[SNARK report: “For Whom Are the Figures Being
Inflated?”’]

[FBIS Translated Text] The total number of Azerbaijani
refugees who abandoned their homes as a result of the
Karabakh conflict constituted 562,000, of whom
477,000 have been settled in Azerbaijan in homes left by
Armenian refugees. This is attested by statistical data
prepared by the Nagorno-Karabakh Republic Ministry
of Foreign Affairs on the basis of material of population
censuses of Azerbaijan and Armenia in recent years,
SNARK reports.

Of the 562,000 Azerbaijani refugees, 154,000 left
Armenia, 38,000 left Nagornyy Karabakh, and 380,000
abandoned areas under the control of the Karabakh
forces, leaving 104,000 homes altogether. At the same
time, on the other hand, 350,000 Armenian refugees,
who left in Azerbaijan more than 90,000 homes, left
Azerbaijan for Armenia. Their homes have provided
living quarters for 477,000 Azerbaijani refugees. At the
same time, only 86,000 Armenian refugees have been
settled in Azerbaijanis’ homes in Armenia, and a further
25,000 Armenians have occupied Azerbaijani homes in
the Nagorno-Karabakh Republic.

According to these figures, 260,000 Armenian and
65,000 Azerbaijani refugees remain homeless at this
time. Thus, the assertions of Baku officials concerning
an alleged 1 million Azerbaijani refugees do not corre-
spond to reality and have been inflated by a factor of at
least 15.
