CIS: Red Tape Blamed for Delays in Integration
MM2008152796 Moscow ROSSIYSKAYA GAZETA
in Russian 17 Aug 96 p 10

[Report by Sergey Vorobyev: “It Is Easier To Reach
Agreement With Foreign Countries Than It Is With the

CIS Countries" — from the “Economic Union” section)

{FBIS Translated Text] The second session of the In-
tegration Committee, which was formed by decision of
the Interstate Council of Customs Union Countries in
the spring of this year, has been held in Moscow. Mem-
bers of the governments of Russia, Belarus, Kazakhstan,
and Kyrgyzstan participated in its work. The Russian

fairs Minister Valeriy Serov. Journalists were briefed
on the problems discusse“ at the Integration Committee
session by its Chairman N.K. Isingarin.

Two Hundred Documents. Zero Commitments

We produce a great number of fine documents, the
committee chairman said. Back in September 1993
nine states, later joined by Georgia, Turkmenistan, and
Ukraine (as associate members), signed the Treaty on
Economic Union. No one remembers it today, but if you
read the treaty you will see for yourselves that you could
not wish for a better organized document. It covers the
whole range of integrational relations among CIS states.

However, three years have passed and no real results
are to be seen.

A plan whereby free trade zones, a customs union, a
payments union...were to be formed was endorsed in
1994, but nothing has been done. Why?

We will never, of course, fully restore all our former
links 2nd Kazakhstan will not manufacture equipment
for submarines, but the preservation of econoniically
valid contacts is vital to us.

Kazakhstan has no one to sell its coal from Ekibastuz to,
except Russia. It is more advantageous for Uzbekistan
to buy cr.nmercial grain from Kazakhstan than Canada.
Russia has always supplied meat to the Urals and
Siberia. Why import iit from Argentina? Who wants
Russian machine tools apart from the former CIS
republics? Zhiguli cars are shipped first and foremost
to the southern republics rather than to countries farther
afield.

There is a danger today that we will go off in different
directions. Some states declare that they want to inte-
grate basically with the West, other states focus their
gaze cn the South.

But who is going to calculate how much time the
restructuring of our countries’ economies will thereby
take? And will the time spent be compensated for by
the new links acquired?

The Arithmetic of Integration

What we need is not political declarations or “higher
mathematics” but the actual “arithmetic” of integration.

We have no problems at the level of heads of govern-
ment. They make very good decisions there.

But at the level of the people wo actually carry them
out we constantly run into bureaucratic obstacles. Deci-
sions are delayed and shelved allegedly because they are
economically inadvisable. The state organs’ task is not
to artificially impose various links on enterprises from
above. We have obtained recommendations from Russia
on forming financial-industrial groups which say that the
head company must be located in Russia and come un-
der Russia for tax purposes, that Kazakhstan must first
and foremost supply raw materials, and so forth. The
models for the proposed financial and industrial groups
copy the models of associations in the former USSR.
However, we think that links should be formed from
below, by the enterprises themselves, while the task of
government structures is to remove barriers to the pro-
cesses of integration and create the requisite conditions.
What is the point, for example, of Kazakhstan and
Uzbekistan joining a “single economic area” if they have
no treaty on the mutual convertibility of their currency?
Neither side has dollars, of course. So how do they
trade?

The CIS’ paradox is that very close neighbors should
be completely unable to solve questions which are
easily solved by countries farther afield. Agreements to

(FBIS Translated Text] Minsk, 20 Aug (ITAR-TASS)
— A meeting of the leaders and specialists of the
transport ministries of Belarus, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan,

transport system in the four countries. Representatives
of these states established the outlines for a future four-

cooperation and the terms for mutual relations in the
transport sector.

It was agreed that the states are to harmonize their ba-

mon technical policy, to agree the principles for setting
charges, and to cooperate closely in international trans-
port.

The future agreement is also to contain standards to
guarantee that documentation certifying qualifi:ations in
the transport sector and driving licences will be equally
valid for all. The next meeting of the transport group,
which was set up by an integration committee decision,
will be he’d in Almaty at the end of September.
