Baltics Cautious About Military Cooperation With
Russia

WS3011121994 Tallinn BNS in English 1805 GMT
29 Nov 94

[FBIS Transcribed Text] Tallinn, Nov 29, BNS—In a
further sign that the Baltics have adopted an attitude of
cautiousness toward possible military cooperation with
Russia, the Baltic defense force leaders at a press confer-
ence after a meeting in Tallinn mentioned no definite
ways how the three nations could work closely with the
huge eastern neighbor.

The Estonian Commander in Chief, Aleksander Einseln,
told reporters on Tuesday there was “no particular
military cooperation” going on between the Baltics and
Russia at the moment. He said cooperation on military
issues could be talked about some time in the future.

His Latvian counterpart, Colonel Juris Dalbinsh, said
extensive military cooperation between Latvia and
Russia was not an issue that could be discussed in the
immediate future. “We can solve certain problems
together, but not cooperate on large scale,” the Latvian
army commander said.

Colonel Antanas Jurgaitis, head of the operative plan-
ning department of the Lithuanian Armed Forces central
staff, was short-spoken, saying Lithuania and Russia
were not cooperating in the field of the military.

The issue of possible military cooperation between
Estonia and Russia came up in April, when Commander
in Chief Major General Aleksander Einseln made an
official visit to Moscow. The Estonian army chief met
several Russian top military and paid a visit to the
Kantermirov division stationed near Moscow.

Einseln said in April Estonian officers could be trained
at Russian military academies. “This would be of advan-
tage to our officers, the way that training in other
countries is,” he told BNS then.

The Estonian commander in chief didn’t rule out that
the country could buy weapons and military equipment
from Russia. He said Estonia was interested in obtaining
tussian-made anti-tank and anti-aircraft weapons. “We
want to have high-quality weapons at a low price. If we
can get them from Russia we will buy weapons from
there,” the general told reporters in Moscow.

Taking part in this scientific-practical conference are

As an Interfax correspondent reports, at the start of the

that although Russia intends to participate in joint CIS
anti-aircraft defense, its leadership has been out of contact
with CIS army commanders on the issue.

Akhmedov also suggested that because of the absence of
Russia’s military command, debates on a number of
important issues should be cancelled.

The Russian delegation at the conference is led by
General Yevgeniy Smirnov, Moscow’s envoy at ‘he
Headquarters for Coordination of Military Cooperation
within CIS.

The conference’s sponsors reported to Interfax that ini-

expected to take part. CIS Coordination Headquarters
explain Gromov’s absence by his possible dismissal from
the office of deputy defense chief. According to their
information, a decree from President Boris Yeltsin could
appear within the next few days providing for a 30 percent
cut in the armed forces central command. It is assumed
that apart from Gromov, two other Deputy Defense Min-
isters, Viadimir Toporov and Valeriy Mironov, will also go
since their offices will be abolished.

The CIS Coordination Headquarters also doubts that

Kozyrev: Russia May Cease Partnership With
Expanding NATO

LD3011210494 Moscow INTERFAX in English 1944
GMT 30 Nov 94

[FBIS Transcribed Text] Russia may give up the pro-
gram of partnership with NATO if that bloc expands,
Foreign Minister Andrey Kozyrev said.

Answering an Interfax question before he flew to Paris
Wednesday [30 November] Kozyrev emphasized that he
might forego the presentation of the Russia-NATO part-
nership program if the NATO Council adopted “a bloc
expansion calendar.”

In his words, “hasty expansion of NATO and scheduling
this process will create a new situation. This will be an
obvious departure from the partnership we agreed on.”

Kozyrev expressed the hope, however, that this would
not happen. He noted that numerous West European
and other countries opposed decisions which could make
cooperation and partnership in Europe more difficult.“

He will attend a West European Council’s meeting In
Paris. He plans to go on to Brussels Thursday afternoon
to submit the Russia- NATO partnership program to the
North Atlantic Cooperation Council meeting.

On December 2 Kozyrev will attend a meeting of the
contact group on Bosnia and Herzegovina and have a
tete-a-tete meting with Secretary of State Warren Chris-
topher.

He said that Russia wanted its partnership with NATO
to expand and become more tangible. He said: “We are
prepared to suggest numerous events in the framework
of the Russia-NATO partnership and of the Partnership
for Peace program so that we could know each other
better and learn to work and live together.”

Afanasyevskiy on NATO Expansion

[FBIS Transcribed Text] Russian Deputy Foreign Min-
ister Nikolay Afanasyevskiy told an Interfax correspon-
dent that the program for Russia-NATO partnership was
practically prepared “though some details b2v< yet to be
coordinated.” The Russian foreign minister is expected
to introduce the program in Brussels where the North
Atlantic Cooperation Council will meet on December 2.

Afanasyevskiy made it clear that the Russia-NATO
program on partnership could be influenced by decisions
of the NATO Council at the level of the foreign minis-
ters. He voiced hope that NATO would not approve the
“schedule for enlarging NATO on which some pro-
Atlantic circles have insisted.”

Afanasyevskiy said he believed that enlargement of
NATO would be a counterproductive move at the
present moment and could seriously complicate work of
the CSCE summit in Budapest.

Afanasyevskiy said the reforms in NATO declared four
years ago were slow. “Even the Russian Armed Forces
adapt themselves to the new reality more quickly though
with greater difficulties,” he added.

Commenting on cooperation between Russia and
NATO, Afanasyevskiy said it must be equal. “‘We are in
favor of transparency and predictability as far as actions
of every party are concerned,” he added.

He said neither Russia nor NATO were ready for
granting to each other the right to veto their actions. He
said it was not the main problem now. “Our partnership
must not involve either vetoes or surprises. The Russia-
NATO program aims directly at this and at bringing our
relations to a quite new level.”

Commenting on the coming CSCE summit in Budapest,
Afanasyevskiy said it could be “a turning point for the
new post-confrontation Europe.” “All the CSCE mem-
ber-states realize the necessity for building a unified
Europe. Discord concerns only the ways for doing this.
In Budapest we will synchronize our watches and decide
where Europe should go,” he said.

Afanasyevskiy said the direction of movement of Europe
would be reflected in the political declaration which the
CSCE summit was expected to approve. The declaration
is called To New Cooperation in the New Epoch.

He also said the CSCE summit was likely to discuss
efficiency of the organization. ““The UN recognized the
CSCE as a regional organization. However, the CSCE
does not yet belong even to this kind of organizations as
its decisions are of political-diplomatic character and are
not juridically obligatory.”

Afanasyevskiy said what was good for the period of bloc
confrontation was not normal now when Europe had to
face such “new challenges as aggressive nationalism,
violation of human rights and rights of ethnic minorities,
etc.” He said the CSCE needed new mechanisms.

At the same time, he stressed that Russia was not ready
for rejecting the principle of consensus for adopting
decisions as some Western countries proposed. He said
Russia believed that t:.e principle must be preserved
until a new basis of the CSCE and new systems of
European security were created.

Afanasyevskiy said he believed that the CSCE summit
would also focus on peacekeeping operations. He said
the issue was of great importance for Russia as the
majority of conflicts in Europe took place on the ex-
USSR territory.

He said Russia could not accept the position of those
Western countries which would like to place its peace-
keeping and mediatory activities under some control. He

{[FBIS Transcribed Text] And now Anatoliy Potapov

And so the program for interaction between Russia and
NATO is almost ready. Moscow has empowered its
ambassador to Belgium, Vitaliy Churkin, to harmonize
with NATO final details in the program. What in general
terms does the program of cooperation between Russia
and NATO represent?

The program consists of two parts. The first part relates
to Russia’s proposals on cooperation with NATO within
the Partnership for Peace concept. It should be recalled
that NATO’s basic document in the above-mentioned
partnership refers only vaguely to cooperation, and each
state participating in the program is allotted its own
concrete proposals. Russia was offered in the main
military cooperation with NATO That kind of cooper-
ation concerns the holding of joint military exercises,

officers spending a probation period with NATO. In a

All the same, in the opinion of Anatoliy Potapov, Rus-

heard]. It goes beyond the Partnership for Peace pro-
gram. It is precisely this wide section that takes into
account Russia’s specifics—its size, influence, and
authority on the international scene and, perhaps most
of all, its nuclear might. Russia’s demand for certain
conditions in its partnership with NATO has been neces-
sitated by all these Russian-specific features. Wisely, the

accepted Russia’s demand.

The above-mentioned section envisages that selfsame

Thus these two sections constitute the individual pro-

Touching on the problem of power shortage, Georgian
Head of State Eduard Shevardnadze said that he was not
satisfied with the performance of the leadership of the
Gruzgaz [Georgian gas] Department, and stressed that
he was forced to personally visit Turkmenistan and ask
its president to postpone the repayment deadline of the
gas debt by Georgia and resume gas supplies to Georgia.
It cannot go on like this, Shevardnadze said, debts must
be repaid.
