Defense Ministers’ Meeting: Grachev Fires
Spokesman

LDO0112133594 Moscow Russian Television Network in
Russian 1100 GMT I Dec 94

[From the “Vesti” newscast]

[FBIS Translated Text] Today’s session of the Council of
CIS Defense Ministers began with insistent calls by
many heads of war departments to defend them from
incompetent attacks in the press.

At the session [Russian Defense Minister] Pavel
Grachev announced the removal of (Serafim Yushkov),
head of the press center of the Staff for Coordination of
Military Cooperation of CIS Member States, from his
post and his transferral to the Administration for Infor-
mation of the Defense Ministry. Yushkov was removed
for exceeding his official powers. It is implied evidently
that he has been punished for his open letter to Boris
Yeltsin in NEZAVISIMAYA GAZETA of 22 October.

In general, the session discussed questions of a military-
political nature, namely, the planned widening of the
powers of the commander of the Collective Peace-
keeping Forces in Tajikistan, material-technical and
financial back up for maintaining peace in the Abkhaz-
Georgian conflict zone, and also reorganization of the
structure of the Staff for Coordinating Military Cooper-
ation.

Grachev on CIS Peacekeeping in Tajikistan,
Abkhazia

LDO112231994 Moscow Radio Rossii Network in
Russian 1900 GMT 1 Dec 94

[FBIS Translated Text] A meeting of the Council of CIS
Defence Ministers took place on Thursday {1 December]
in the Moscow headquarters for coordinating military
cooperation. In an interview the Russian minister Pavel
Grachev who chaired the meeting told journalists what
he thought were the most important issues which had
been discussed. They include increasing the powers of
the commander of the CIS collective peacekeeping forces
in the republic of Tajikistan. At present these duties are
performed by Lieutenant-General Valeriy Patrikeyev.

[Begin Grachev recording] The main point of the addi-
tional powers is that if an emergency situation arises on
a particular section in Tajikistan Lieutenant-General
Patrikeyev has the right to take the decision autono-
mously to use peacekeeping forces and then, subse-
quently, to inform and submit a report to the defense
ministers of the states whose peacekeeping forces are
used. The second aspect is to introduce structural or
organizational and manning level changes to the struc-
ture of the management apparatus of the peacekeeping
forces and to allow him to recommend the appointment
or dismissal of those officers and generals whom he
considers it necessary to dismiss. [end recording]

INTERREGIONAL AFFAIRS 1

Grachev said that a solution has also been found to the
question of financing the CIS collective peacekeeping
forces’ operation in Abkhazia. The minister expressed
satisfaction that at present there is considerably less
dispute about this question. He also noted that Ukraine
which did not sign the Collective Security Treaty also
expressed the wish to make a contribution, within its
means, to restoring peace in this hot spot.

Council Discusses Promotion of Economic
Cooperation

LD0112195794 Moscow ITAR-TASS in English 1719
GMT I Dec 94

[By BELINFORM correspondent Viktor Sakhankov for
TASS]

[FBIS Transcribed Text] Minsk December 1—The CIS
Intergovernmental Council has set up an operating
council on agroindustrial secter and an intergovern-
mental science and technology centre as a move to
promote economic cooperation between the CIS states.

The Intergovernmental Council met in Minsk today.
Two items on the agenda were the agrarian policy and
agricultural economic reforms, and cooperation in con-
trol of animal and plant parasites and diseases.

These challenges are urgent, with the CIS’ agricultural
sectors plagued by decline in production and monetary
systems and by climbing energy prices.

Opposes NATO’s Extension to East

LD0112190894 Moscow ITAR-TASS in English 1832
GMT | Dec 94

[By ITAR-TASS correspondents Mikhail Kalmykov,
Andrey Krasnoshchekov, Yuriy Lopatin, and Aleksey
Poroshin]

[FBIS Transcribed Text] Paris December | TASS—The
development of multi-aspect partnership is the most
straightforward way to overcome mutual distrust,
existing in Russia’s relations with NATO and other
West-European agencies. This distrust is a remainder of
the past, Russian Foreign Minister Andrey Kozyrev told
participants in the 40th session of the Western European
Union’s assembly here on Thursday [1 December].

At the session, Kozyrev described Russia’s stance on the
European security problems.

Asked about the Russian attitude to NATO’s possible
extending to the east, Kozyrev said that “this problem is
to be solved by these (East-European) countries them-
selves”. In Russia’s view, however, one must not speed
up this process, as the current stage offers good prospects
for East-European countries’ interaction with NATO
within the Partnership for Peace program.

This will enable (the concerned states) to overcome
mutual suspicions and specify spheres and forms of
cooperation. It would be appropriate io decide on this or
that country’s joining NATO only after a “preparatory
period’’, the Russian foreign minister said.

Russia does not view NATO as a potential enemy.
However, we are talking about enlarging a military and
political organization, which may bring its eastern bor-
ders much closer to Russia, and this can stir up a certain
concern in the Russian public. Therefore, “‘one need not
hurry with extending NATO. Haste will make no good”,
he said.

Touching upon a forthcoming Budapest summit of the
Conference for Security and Cooperation in Europe,
Kozyrev said Russia believes the CSCE must play central
part in the European process due to its universal nature.

The CSCE’s original goal was to unite all the European
countries. At the modern stage, it should become an
efficient international organization, with its charter, as
well as legal and institutional support for peace-making
operations and protection of human rights. The CSCE
authority should be also used to prevent the develop-
ment of aggressive nationalism, the Russian minister
said.

Speaking about a suggested revision of the nuclear Non-
Proliferation Treaty, Kozyrev said Russia stands for the
only amendment to the document, that is to make it
infinite. At the same time, Russian proposals on further
reduction of Russian and U.S. nuclear arsenals are still
in force, Kozyrev noted.

Asked to comment upon the last French initiative to
hold an international conference on the Bosnian settle-
ment, Kozyrev said “one should first decide on what to
speak about”, and after that discuss whether this forum
would be appropriate.

He noted that the Contact Group members have repeat-
edly debated a possible composition of such a gathering.

Russia thinks a “G-8” format could be used, although
any other number of participants might be good as well.
However, some partners in the Contact Group oppose its
enlargement for fear that the group will turn into some
sort of a discussion club.

Upon reporters’ requesi, Kozyrev briefed them on the
situation in Chechnya. He said that constitutional rights
of the population are being infringed on this territory,
and criminal groups use growing military confrontation
to achieve their own purposes.

Speaking at the news conference A. Kozyrev highly
commended the work that had been done on the docu-
ment, which he had been ready to adopt. But literally just
two hours previously he had received the communique
on the NATO Council session. The first impression was
that “it contains more questions than answers.”

One gets the impression from this document that a
tendency toward quick preparations for an expansion of
NATO is discernible in it. A deadline has been set for
completing “internal discussion,” while no deadlines are
specified regarding cooperation in the sphere of “Part-
nership,” which Russia regards as the priority.

Kozyrev highly appreciated NATO’s desire, reflected in
the communique, to build Europe without dividing lines.
“But we need time to clarify the issues which have
arisen. We hope to go back to the path of partnership and
to adopt these two programs,” he said. ““We can see no
alternative to cooperation with Western democracies
and we will try to achieve equal partnership with them,”
he said.

[FBIS Translated Text] Vladimir Lukin, 57, chairman of
the Russian State Duma Foreign Affairs Committee
(former ambassador to the United States), who is visiting
Japan, gave an interview to YOMIURI SHIMBUN in
Tokyo on 30 November. He discussed how to deal with
the stalemate in the Bosnia-Hercegovina conflict.

Regarding the recent development in which the “Liaison
Group,” formed by the United States, Russia, Britain,
France, and Germany, began to consider amending the
peace plan presented to the conflicting parties in the
Bosnian conflict during their negotiations with Serbian
President Slobodan Milosevic, Lukin sternly criticized
the Western nation’s “policy change.” He said: “The
Western nations have always treated Milosevic like a
criminal, regarding him the culprit who aggravated the
conflict. Now they realize that there is nobody they can
turn to except him.”

With regard to the amended peace plan including a
federation scheme between New Yugoslavia (consisting
of Serbia and Montenegro) and Serbian forces in Bosnia,
Lu'.n expressed a positive view, saying: “This should
have been deliberated on, while taking into consider-
ation the rights of the minorities, before the conflict had
intensified.”

Commenting on the discord among Western nations
over how to deal with the Bosnian conflict, Lukin said:
“Priority should be ;ven to collective diplomatic efforts
to stop the fighting and serarate the forces. Whether or
not Russia can play a constructive role also depends on
whether Western nations can agree among themselves.”
He called for a level-headed approach.

On the NATO forces’ intensifying its armed intervention
in the Bosnian conflict, Lukin stated: “It is still not
realistic for an organization that was formed to challenge
the old Warsaw Treaty Organization to find its raison
d’etre in intervention in regional conflicts, which it has
never experienced in the past. It is useless to do futile
things.” He indicated that NATO’s military intervention
does not help resolve the conflict.

Cooperation With WEU Seen as Way to
European Peace

[FBIS Transcribed Text] Paris December 1 TASS—
Russia is regarding its cooperation with the Western
European Union (WEU) as a perspective means of
strengthening peace in Europe, Russian Foreign Minister
Andrey Kozyrev stressed in his speech at a WEU’s
parliamentary assembly in Paris today where he was
invited by the leadership of this organization.

Such interaction, he said, means not a policy “against”
whoever it may be but a policy “in favor”—for the
realization of the interests of security of Russia, other
European countries and all Russia’s Western partners
including the United States. However, “the fact remains
that we, the Europeans, should take care of ourselves in
the first place,” the Russian foreign minister stressed.

According to the head of the Russian foreign office, a
dialogue between Russia and the Western European
Union should be orientated towards a more active search
for common approaches to the key problems of security
in Europe and neighboring regions.

Addressing parliamentarians of the WEU’s countries he
emphas.sed: ““You may be sure of a consistent, peaceful
and democratic policy of Russia’”’.

Andrey Kozyrev noted with satisfaction that Russian
and WEU approaches to the Bosnian crisis were “very
close”. Russia stands for the settlement of this problem
by political means, he said.

It is necessary to give a firm rebuff to any violators of
U.N. Security Council resolutions. Sanctions should be
applied strictly within the framework of decisions by the
Security Council and under its control. Touching on the
decision by Belgrade to close the border with Bosnia, the
Russian minister spoke for a “‘stage-by-stage lifting of
sanctions against rump Yugoslavia”.

Russia and the Western European Union, the top Rus-
sian diplomat noted, are sharing a common viewpoint
that “lifting of the embargo on arms deliveries to Mos-
lems will only lead to the further escalation of the
conflict”.

A situation may arise in which the U.N. peacekeeping
forces may be withdrawn from Bosnia. Russia, like
Britain and France, would pull out its peacekeepers if the
arms embargo on Bosnia were lifted and if the situation
developed into an uncontrollable escalation of the con-
flict, Andrey Kozyrev warned.

The foreign minister read out at the session a decree by
Russian President Boris Yeltsin on the imposition as of
today of three-year moratorium on export of antiper-
sonnel mines not equipped with a mechanism for self-
destruction and not to discovered by mine detectors.

According to Andrey Kozyrev, this Russian initiative
must make a contribution to the overcoming of tragic
consequences 0: armed conflicts in different parts of the
planet.

A Single Social Payment Is Needed

The diaft speaks of the need for the consolidation of
extrabudgetary funds—tne social and medical insurance
and employment funds—in the budget.

A “centralization” of this kind is fraught with far-
reaching consequences and is contrary to the interests of

Furthermore, the complicated system of payments is
reinforced even though those p2yments have a single
revenue base [raschetnaya baza]—the labor remunera-
tion fund. To reduce the number of taxes and payments,
cut bureaucracy and red tape, and reduce the cost of
collection it is expedient to amalgamate these contribu-
tions into a single social payment which would then be
divided among the various funds according to legally
enshrined normatives.

It is necessary to introduce the principle of “‘o: tax—
one payment,” a principle generally recognized }.. world
practice.

Punishing the Law-Abiding

In an overall assessment of the tax reform proposed by
the draft budget the following should be noted.

The reduction in the total number of taxes is insignifi-
cant, in other words, the unwieldiness of the system itself
is not being eliminated. The old format of setting,
levying, and paying taxes is being preserved (with a
certain strengthening of sanctions on nonpayers). This
will be a further incentive to the proliferation of super-
fluous paperwork in the economy, will slow down gen-
eral money circulation, and increase the cost of the tax
collection system and the maintenance of the taxation

organs.

As an alternative there could be a system in which taxes
are paid to the federal, regional, and local budgets in
accordance with the legally established normatives.

The draft contains tough sanctions for nonpayment of
taxes. It is clear, however, that there may be different
reasons for nonpayment—from simple mathematical
error to varying interpretations of the normative docu-
ments.

The proposed sanctions will be aimed primarily at
law-abiding tax payers and will have no effect on those
who make extensive efforts to conceal the taxable
sources by making big transactions in cash.

“Tilegal” payments in cash have become widespread
primarily because of the inordinately high rates of tax
and are a consequence of the emergence in the economy
of a parallel “shadow” form of money circulation not
controlled by the state. Payments in cash are undoubt-
edly one of the main reasons for the colossal shortfall in
revenue to the state budget including revenue for social
needs.

In addition, a practice has developed recently in which
the financial organs’ normative documents have been
obliging tax payers to “split” their taxes among the
federal, regional, and local budgets. As a result an
enterprise is forced to fill out several payment docu-
ments when paying a single tax.

On a national scale this has led to the vast and unwar-

“Starvatiea Diet” Prescribed for The Social Sphere

The draft budget’s considerable shortcomings are con-
nected with the policy of funding the social sphere. The
main shortcoming is the absence of clearly defined
priorities. Although priorities were outlined in the 1994
budget, they have not been implemented.

Meanwhile, one of the main priorities is the funding of

guarantees ensured from the federal budget’s resources.

needs developed in recent years will be fully retained in

This relates primarily tc the introduction of needs allow-

Even though we have not yet reached the end of 1994,

of probability that the same fate awaits these items of
expenditure in the 1995 budget.

The trend toward the decentralization of social spending
is being consolidated. Put more simply, the burden of
spending on the social sphere is being shifted to the local
budgets. Consequently the local organs of power will
bear the responsibility for the efficient functioning of the
social sphere at a time of limited financial potential
because of the existing and planned distribution of taxes.

This enables the clear conclusion to be drawn that the
financial normatives on which the budget is based will
not be sufficient to ensure the stabilization of the
funding of the social sphere with all the attendant
socioeconomic and political consequences and that they
therefore need radical reworking.

Russian Community Says Cultural Needs
‘Disregarded’

WS$2911105194 Tallinn BNS in English 1815 GMT 28
Nov 94

{FBIS Transcribed Text] Vilnius, Nov 28, BNS—The
Lithuanian Government does not support the develop-
ment of Russian culture in the country and even seeks to
create an “information and cultural vacuum,” the Rus-
sian Community of Lithuania (RCL) said in a commu-
nique released Nov. 26.

Last week, RCL representatives met with Vasili Tara-
senko, chairman of the Federation Affairs Committee of
the Russian Federation Council (upper house of the
Russian parliament).

The meeting focused on problems encountered by Rus-
sians living in Lithuania and their aspiration to get
integrated into Lithuania’s political, economic and
social life without losing their language and culture.
RCL representatives indicated that the cultural needs of
Russians are disregarded in Lithuania, the number of
radio and TV programs in Russian is reduced and that
Russian schools are in a critical situation through
shortage of funds.

Tarasenko said preparatory work for supporting Rus-
sians in the Baltic countries and the CIS is currently
underway on governmental and legislative level in
Russia. He also promised to inform Lithuania’s Govern-
ment about problems pointed out during the meeting.
