Russia: Yeltsin Warns on Consequences of
Zyuganov, Zhirinovskiy

LD2604141196 Moscow INTERFAX in English
1224 GMT 26 Apr 96

{FBIS Transcribed Text] MOSCOW, April 26 (Interfax)
— President Boris Yeltsin believes that Russia will
have enough wisdom “not to allow the re-installation
in this country of an administrative command system,
not to Mention the restoration of the ways of the Soviet
system.”

The president answered earlier faxed questions of jour-
nalists from the Ivanovo region that he does not believe
in the gradual halt of the country’s market economy.
“We would have to pay too high a price,” he said, “all
the way to civil war, the collapse of government, in-
ternational isolation, and the reversion of Russia to the
ogre of the civilized world.’

Yeltsin is sure that the people, “having visited most of
the circles of Bolshevik Hell — repressions, hunger,
rationing, spiritual degradation — will not voluntarily
submit again to the Communist yoke and kneel again
on their knees.”

“Will our country be democratic?” is the question facing
our country, Yeltsin said. “Will we respect human
rights? Will we have freedom? Will this freedom be
guaranteed by the firmness of the constitution and the
inviolability of private property? Will social security for
the poor be guaranteed? Will our government be a full-
fledged member of the international community?”

PRESIDENTIAL ELECTION ISSUES

Yeltsin is doing all he can to prevent the triumph
of Communist leader Gennadiy Zyuganov or ultra-
nationalist Vladimir Zhirinovskiy at the June 16 elec-
tions. “Both will be catastrophic for Russia. The first,
perhaps after some time, the second — immediately,”
Yeltsin said.

Asked about the prospects for Russia's struggling textile
industry (which is led by Ivanovo), ‘Yeltsin answered
sincerely that he would not “make baseless promises”
in order to gain votes “the way some of my opponents
are.

But he did say he saw several major directions for
solving the industry's woes. "We need to develop and
simplify our export-import operations, strengthen ties
for raw materials from cotton-producing countries, and
constantly provide the industry with raw materials,” he
said. Russia is already developing a complex of factory-
towns, and Yeltsin will “defend the competitiveness
of Russian producers, especially in the technology
field, which will include conversion from the military
industry,” he said.

He stressed that his recent order on property rights was
meant “neither to demolish nor recreate the collective
farm system.” Instead, he wanted to “begin changing
the psychology of the farmers, back to their former
closeness with the land,” he said. "We need to have
the hope of guaranteeing our future and our children’s
future, and the ownership of land is one of the most
reliable guarantees for a person's well-being.

(FBIS jrans!ated Excerpt] Moscow is once again
preparing to mark May Day “just as before” — with
processions, outdoor parties, and merriment. This

was to the liking, it would appear, not only of [NEZA-

was no mere bystander either. [passage omitted]

The people want calm and stability in their everyday
lives and confidence in the future. They want strong
government, but not drastic measures, even if the
architects of new revolutionary theories promise to show
the world “the miracle of Russian spiritual revival.”

It was this very nostalgia that partly secured victory for
the Communists in the parliamentary elections last year.
Boris Yeltsin has drawn the conclusions and organized
“therapy” — he has introduced order over the issue
of pay, begun to eradicate delays in the payment of
pensions, and signed edicts on increasing the minimum
pension, on land, on restoring savings, on helping the
agricultural complcx, and so forth. Speaking at the
congress of his supporters’ movement, Boris Yeltsin
named the family as his major task for his second
presidential term. Not the abstract “society,” or “great
Russia,” or “statehood,” or a “superpower,” but the
family has been chosen by the president as the main
focus of his attention. He assured his supporters that
he would consider the welfare and prosperity of the
ordinary family as the main yardstick of his activity.

This election guideline is having an extremely sobering
effect on the electorate, who are nostalgic for the
stagnation era. The people have already begun to realize
that what they want is not the stagnation era in its
entirety, but “the stagnation era with beer and sausage
meat, but no Communists.” Boris Yeltsin has understood
this and taken account of it in his election campaign.
It is no accident that the president has also restored
state rights to the Banner of Victory. that banner of the
reddest hue under which the majonty of our bygone
May Days were passed

1438 GMT 26 Apr 96

(FBIS Transcribed Text] MOSCOW, April 26 (Interfax)
— Ex-Soviet President and Russian presidential candi-
date Mikhail Gorbachev has suggested that such issues
as the war in Chechnya, possible steps toward CIS in-
tegration and financial stability be left outside the pres-
idential campaign.

“There must be no speculation on these topics because
they are very serious,” he told a news conference in
Moscow Friday.

Gorbachev confirmed that if he is elected president, he
“will not use the constitutional right to disband the State
Duma and will do everything to develop constructive
cooperation” with parliament.

He recalled that while meeting with Kemerovo regional
parliament speaker Aman Tuleyev, he upheld his pro-
posal that all presidential candidates sign an agreement
stating that they “favor free elections and will respect
the will of the people, no matter who is elected presi-
dent.”

After his Siberian tour, Gorbachev concluded that “60
to 70 percent of voters have not decided for whom to
cast their ballots,” regarding the candidacies of both
the incumbent Russian President Boris Yeltsin and
leader of the Communist Party Gennadiy Zyuganov as
unacceptable.

Gorbachev believes that if there is no adequate public
reaction to the April 24 assault on him as a presidential
candidate, when he was struck on the head by a resident
of Omsk, such incidents may undermine the presidential

campaign.

He also said that now that Chechen separatist leader
Dzhokhar Dudayev is dead, the Chechen side will
be less willing to compromise. While Dudayev had a
certain degree of control over the rebels. there is now a
threat that the separatists will choose what Gorbachev
