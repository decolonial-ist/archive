1835 GMT 13 Jul 96

{By Valentin Volkov]

{[FBIS Transcribed Text] THE HAGUE, July 13 (Itar-
Tass) - Belarus has become the 56th country to ratify
the Convention on the Prohibition of the Development,
Production, Stockpiling and Use of Chemical Weapons.

The instrument of ratification has been handed over to
U.N. Secretary General Butrus Butrus-Ghali who is the
depository of the convention.

The convention which was opened for signing on
January 13, 1993 will become effective 180 days after
the 65th country transfer its instrument of ratification to
the depository.

At present, the convention has been signed by 160
countries and ratified by 56 countries.

Lukashenka, who is the French capital on an official
visit, said that was the first comprehensive treaty Belarus
had signed with a major European nation.

The Belarussian leader hopes that the treaty would fur-
nish the legal basis for further cooperation between the
two countries and start systematic bilateral consultations
on European security.

Asked about the importance of the treaty, experts in the
Belarussian delegation said that it provided for cooper-
ation between the Belarussian and French parliaments
and promoted business contacis and investments.

The treaty specifies cooperation in spheres having
particular i to both countries. For instance,
the article provides for cooperation in easing and
eliminating the effects of the Chernoby! nuclear power
plant disaster.

Before the treaty was signed Lukashenka and Chirac
held talks. Chirac told journalists afterwards that it
was an important meeting, because it helped make
an important step towards friendship between the two
countries.

Both men pointed to an inadequate level of political,
economic and cultural relations between France and
Belarus and the need for developing diversified relations
between the two peoples.

European security was one of the issues discussed. The
presidents have a similar attitude to the problem.

The conclusion of the full-scale treaty of friendship has
laid the basis of new relations between Belarus and
France, President Chirac said.

The French president promised to pay a return visit
when it would become possible.

In turn Lukashenka said he was satisfied with the fact
that the president of France had a chance to see for
himself that in his country the policies of the Belarussian
leadership had been presented in the distorted fashion.

Lukashenka hopes that President Chirac will be able
to visit Minsk in the near future. “Probably, this will
happen very soon,” he said.

“The importance of the meeting with President Chirac
is hard to underestimate, and the Belarussian people
know how to be grateful. This is our national trait,”
Lukashenka said.

As he reviewed Lukashenka’s talks with Chirac, first

aide Ural Latypov said that relations with
France in the West were no less important to Belarus
than with Russia in the East. It was agreed that France
would help Belarus join the European Union.

Latypov said that the Belarussian and Preach
had largely similar views on the future of NATO.

“The alliance must be reformed and NATO's expansion
to the East must not be fast," Latypov said.

Chirac said he wanted NATO's expansion to be carried
in such a way so as to prevent the infringement of the
interests of Russia, Belarus and NATO. Latypov quoted
Chirac as saying that the simple expansion NATO,
which had been established to counter the USSR, was
unnecessary. NATO must be reformed, he said.

In the course of the talks the French President said
that the question of Belarussian sovereignty within the
framework of integration between Belarus and Russia
was beyond all doubt.

“Belarus is a sovereign, independent country,” Latypov
quoted Chirac as saying.

As he addressed France's leading bankers and industri-
alists later in the day, Lukashenka said that the CIS was
a military, political and economic club, which gathered
from time to time but never acted on its decisions.

He believes that France's interest towards Belarus
would be equivalent to nothing, but for the “union of
two” (Russia and Belarus) and “the union of four” (Rus-
sia, Belarus, Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan) concluded
within the CIS. Lukashenka believes that both unions
give French businessmen a chance to open joint ventures
in Belarus to manufacture goods and sell them without
any problems in the territories of the mentioned states.

Lukashenka said that in Belarus a favorable investment
climate had been established by virtue of the country's
geographic position, developed infrastructures, inexpen-
sive fuel from Russia, skilled workforce and legal ben-
efits for foreign investors.

Lukashenka says that the integration of Belarus and
Russia is the highest in the post-Soviet space. "We have
been coordinating economic reforms, unifying legal
systems, consulting with each other on foreign policy
issues, in a word, acting in the same fashion as the EU
member-countries,” Lukashenka said.

At the same time Lukashenka confessed that the inte-
gration of Belarus and Russia was still far behind that
of the European Union.

Lukashenka invited French businessmen to invest into
the Belarussian economy. “My visit to Paris will serve
this process,” Lukashenka said. He hopes France would
open a $200- 300 million credit line to finance projects
launched by French companies in Belarus.

France's biggest insurance company COFAS has agreed
to act as a safeguard of that plan.

security guarantees program for the Baltic states. The
United States plans to get the program ready till the

also political significance because they underline the
Baltic countries’ belonging to the European political
structure.

Krastins emphasized the role of the Baltic Challenge
in Latvia's further course toward international security
structures.

ability to cooperate with the military units of other
Baltic countries and the United States, said the minister.
